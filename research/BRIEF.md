## PART A -- FACTS

### PBR: the myth and the business

1. Pabst tied **real blue silk ribbons** on bottle necks from **1882** -- eleven years before
   the fair. The Pabst Mansion says they used **1 million feet of ribbon a year**. It was
   retail decoration, never a prize.
2. The **1893 World's Columbian Exposition awarded no ribbons.** Judges scored against a fixed
   standard, not head to head; **every beer scoring 80 percent or above got an identical bronze
   St Gaudens medal**. No gold, no silver, no grand prize, no ribbons. Pabst got the same medal
   as everyone else and marketed itself as the winner. (Dr Loih, 2017, from the 1891 contest
   rules and Bancroft's *Book of the Fair*.) The Pabst Mansion and the Wisconsin Historical
   Society both revised their websites afterwards.
3. The slogan **"Selected as America's Best in 1893"** was written in **the 1950s** by a
   struggling Pabst. Sixty years separate the event from the claim about the event.
4. Dates: founded Milwaukee **1844**; renamed Pabst **1890**; peak **18 million barrels, 1977**;
   sold to Kalmanovitz **1985** for **63 million dollars**; **Milwaukee plant closed 1996** and
   Pabst became a **"virtual brewer"** with no plant of its own; bottom **below 1 million
   barrels, 2001**; sold to Metropoulos **2010** for **250 million**; to Blue Ribbon
   Intermediate Holdings (Eugene Kashper, TSG Consumer Partners) **November 2014** for
   **700 million**.
5. Kalmanovitz's 1985 strategy was to buy dying brands, **cut advertising to zero**, cut quality
   control, milk the residual base. The "we don't advertise" cool began as neglect. The hipster
   revival monetised an accident.
6. **The turnaround was found, not made.** Pabst hired **Neal Stewart** c.2000-01; in **2002**
   sales spiked in **Portland, Oregon** with no marketing behind them, and he went to find out
   why. **2002 was the first volume increase since 1978. 2003 was up 15 percent.**
7. Documented tactics: no TV, no NFL, no FM radio. Bike messenger races, scooter rallies,
   kickball, fashion shows, **art exhibition openings** -- beer and hats delivered to gallery
   openings. Weekly quarter-page ads in alternative papers in about **25 markets**, each listing
   local bands, **never the beer**. MarketingSherpa: "slipping money in the manner of a sly
   uncle to these small events."
8. **Pabst pays artists by name, on the can.** The **PBR Art Can Contest**, from **2014**: over
   **300 million 16 oz cans** carrying contest art, over **100,000 dollars** paid to emerging
   artists. First winner **Josh Holland** (2014). 2021 winner **Ashley Dreyfus**, a Boise
   **mixed-media artist**, whose can ran on **30 million-plus cans**. 2022: **Hannah Bunzey**
   beat about **7,000 entrants from 120-plus countries**.
9. Pabst has sold cases as large as **180 cans**.
10. **Now declining, badly.** Pabst Brewing Co: **5th-largest US brewer in 2024**, **3.1 million
    barrels** (all brands, not PBR alone), **minus 5.6 percent on 2023**, **1.6 percent** of the
    US beer category (Brewers Association). **2025: 18 percent of the workforce cut**, CEO and
    CFO replaced (**Greig DeBow** from 11 August 2025), then **60-70 more** cut in December 2025;
    two former employees estimate **one third to one half** of headcount gone. HQ being sublet;
    a potential class action filed.
11. **PBR Hard Coffee** launched summer 2019, **discontinued October 2022**, sales down
    **40 percent** while the category fell 33. Plus Extra, Easy, Light (2025, rollout "flat"),
    Non-Alc, whiskey, seltzer, THC seltzer. **A brand whose only asset was not being sold has
    spent a decade trying to sell itself.**

### The Polish inversion

12. **PBR sold in Poland was brewed in Poland** by **Van Pur S.A. under licence from Pabst**, at
    **Rakszawa**, Podkarpackie. Launch confirmed **12 May 2020** by **Tomasz Kopyra**, Poland's
    best-known beer blogger, who framed it against **Ciechan**, the previous Polish hipster beer.
13. **Van Pur is the largest independent brewer in Poland**, five breweries. Rakszawa is "the
    heart of Van Pur," largest capacity, **1.75 million hectolitres a year**.
14. Spec: **pale lager, 4.7 percent, 500 ml** non-returnable bottles (one listing says 4.6;
    sources differ by 0.1pp).
15. **The Polish beer press gave it 2 out of 10.**
16. **Price 3.99 to 4.59 zloty**, in **Polomarket, Carrefour, Frisco, Spar** -- mainstream
    grocery, not import shops. **The same price band as ordinary Polish lager.**
17. **Germany:** about **3.00 euro per 355 ml can, 8.45 euro per litre**. **UK:** Trembling
    Madness sells it labelled **"USA Trash Lager."**
18. **China:** Pabst sells **"Blue Ribbon 1844"** -- 720 ml, all-malt reddish-brown strong ale,
    dry-hopped Cascade, aged in **new uncharred American whiskey barrels**, about **44 dollars a
    bottle, served with a champagne flute.**
19. So one trademark is simultaneously a 44-dollar luxury ale, an 8.45 euro-per-litre
    affectation, a 4 zloty supermarket lager from Rakszawa, and a working-class signifier in
    Portland.
20. **Current Polish availability: UNVERIFIED / LIKELY DISCONTINUED.** Safe claim only: PBR was
    licence-brewed in Poland from 2020; whether it still is in 2026 could not be confirmed.

### Polish drinking: the stereotype is quantitatively wrong

21. **Poland is 8th in Europe.** WHO GHO SDG 3.5.2, total per capita 15+, 3-year average, 2024:
    Romania **17.07**, Latvia 14.18, Portugal 13.75, **Czechia 13.52**, Bulgaria 13.25, Spain
    12.07, Austria 11.60, **POLAND 11.51**, Hungary 11.36, Lithuania 11.23, France 11.19,
    **Germany 10.93**, Ireland 10.27.
22. **And falling.** WHO recorded 15+: **2019 = 10.99, the true modern peak**; 2023 = 10.00;
    **2024 = 9.79**. Across 2013-2023 Poland fell **10.81 to 10.00**. The all-time recorded peak
    was **1981 at 11.21**, before the martial-law collapse to 8.54 in 1982.
23. **Two figures, never mixed.** GUS/KCPU counts registered sales over the **whole population**:
    **2024 = 8.8 litres**. WHO counts **15+ and adds unrecorded**: 9.79 recorded, 11.51 total.
24. **The vodka-to-beer shift** (WHO recorded, 15+): **1990 spirits 5.06 L = 62.0 percent**, beer
    2.02 L = 24.8. **2000: spirits 2.47 L = 30.8, beer 3.78 L = 47.1.** **2024: spirits 3.99 L =
    40.8, beer 5.04 L = 51.5.** Beer in natural litres per head: 38.6 (1992), 66.9 (2000),
    **100.5 in 2018, the all-time peak**, about 85.8 (2024).
25. **The 2002 excise cut is the hinge.** Excise on ethyl alcohol cut **30 percent in 2002**;
    spirits went 1.7 L pure per head (2002) to 3.4 (2008) -- **a doubling in six years.**
26. **A Polish minimum wage bought 32 half-litre bottles of vodka in 2001 and 107 in 2021.**
27. **119,598 alcohol retail outlets in 2024 -- one per 302 residents, against one pharmacy per
    2,700.**
28. **The cost, stated once and honestly.** Alcoholic liver cirrhosis deaths 1999 to 2022: men
    45-64, **436 to 2,583**; women 65+, **11 to 436**. Cumulative 1999-2022: **79,000 deaths.**
29. **The Polish beer market is shrinking:** 41 m hl (2018) to **34.6 m hl (2024)**, "the
    equivalent of six large breweries closing." Beer excise **up 47 percent since 2019**. Poland
    is **3rd in the EU for beer production**. Kompania Piwowarska is Asahi, Zywiec is Heineken;
    **Van Pur is Polish-owned.**
30. **Price spread, dated:** 500 ml at **1.99 PLN** in a Biedronka promotion (August 2026) or
    **0.99** at Dino, against **10-15 PLN** in a central Warsaw bar (January 2026). A 100 ml
    **malpka** of vodka is about **8 PLN** -- one bar beer, four times the alcohol.

### The state built the supply

31. **Przymus propinacyjny** is documented: the feudal obligation on peasants to buy the alcohol
    produced on the lord's estate, alongside **przymus konsumpcyjny**, requiring set annual
    quantities. **Polish drinking has a coercive economic skeleton, not merely a convivial one.**
    The **karczma** rivalled the Church as a village institution, hosting weddings, christenings
    and funerals. **Beyond this, the dossiers verify no detailed propinacja or partition-era
    alcohol history. Do not improvise one.**
32. **The Panstwowy Monopol Spirytusowy was founded in 1924** by the Second Republic. The word
    **monopolowy** comes from it.
33. **The best PRL image in the file:** in the **1980s** a rule banned alcohol sales before
    **13:00** while monopolowy shops typically **opened at 10:00** -- a shop selling nothing but
    alcohol, legally forbidden to sell anything for the first three hours of its day.
34. **Alcohol ration cards date from martial law, 13 December 1981**, in a system reaching a
    scope "considerably wider than under the German occupation." Mechanic: **the cards only
    entitled you to buy; you still paid with ordinary money.**
35. **The Soviet three-man ritual was manufactured by decree.** A **December 1958** decree banned
    sale of vodka **by the glass**, so drinkers "were now forced to buy a whole bottle and look
    for chance companions." After the **1961** reform a half-litre cost **2 roubles 87 kopecks**
    -- **one rouble each**, **13 kopecks** over. With no matching glasses they poured
    **"po bulkam,"** by the glugs. By the early 1970s vodka cost 3.62 and the arithmetic broke;
    the custom outlived its own economics.
36. **Schrad's mechanism, and it is Russian.** Ivan the Terrible imported the **kabak** from Kazan
    in **1552**; the **1649** Law Code that tied the peasant to the land also banned selling vodka
    outside it. By the mid-19th century the monopoly was **over a third of imperial revenue** and
    **100 percent of the army's operating budget**. His cross-national table (Foreign Policy,
    26 September 2021): liquor taxes were **26 percent of state income in tsarist Russia,
    25 in the United States, 23 in Britain, 20 in the German Empire, 15 in Sweden and Belgium,
    11 in France and Norway, 9 in Switzerland and Austria-Hungary.** Do not merge the one-third
    with the 26 percent; different years, and he does not date the second.
37. **Policy now, August 2026.** The **oplata malpkowa** is **25 PLN per litre of pure alcohol**
    in packaging up to 300 ml, unchanged since **1 January 2021**. Excise rose **5 percent on
    1 January 2026, not 15**, because **Nawrocki vetoed the larger rise on 18 December 2025** --
    the same date brought plus 20 percent on cigarettes and plus 50 on e-liquids. Night bans:
    **Krakow city-wide since 1 July 2023** (alcohol-related police interventions down
    **48.5 percent** in year one, **61.8** in year two); **Warsaw city-wide from 1 June 2026**,
    22:00-06:00, passed 57 to 2, sole exemption the airport duty-free. A **total ban on all
    alcohol advertising including beer and 0.0 percent**, fines to **1 million PLN**, cleared the
    Health Committee in July 2026 and **awaits second reading. It is not law.** Note the
    collision: it would make an alcohol-brand mural illegal.

### The mural trade

38. **Paint sets the season.** Acrylic facade paint (Sniezka) needs **plus 10 to plus 30 C**;
    silicone (Caparol Muresko) works to **plus 5 C**. No rain, wind, frost, high humidity or
    harsh sun. **The Polish exterior season is roughly six to seven months.** A **1,000 m2 gable
    takes about 170 to 330 litres** of base paint for two coats before any spray work.
39. **Access:** **podnosniki koszowe**, boom lifts, whose **operator must hold UDT certification**.
    Scaffolding is uneconomic on tall buildings. Durations: 50-100 m2 is **4-10 days**. Real jobs:
    the **24-storey Witcher mural in Lodz, over 70 m, took eight painters more than two weeks**
    (October 2021, Jakub Rebelka); **Inti's 11-storey Lodz wall took over 11 days solo** (2013).
40. **Money.** Exterior commercial murals: **200 to 1,000 PLN per m2**, from 200 above 150 m2;
    100 m2-plus jobs **from 20,000 PLN**; **8 percent VAT** on works of art. Good Looking Studio,
    the market leader, refuses a public price list. **These are client-side contractor prices,
    not artist take-home** -- paint, lift hire with a licensed operator, insurance, assistants and
    **unpaid design and permit time that can run months** come out of them. **Poland has no
    published muralist day rate and no interview in which a Polish muralist states their own fee.**
    Income stabilises off the wall: the named artists hold academic posts (**Waras at ASP Gdansk,
    Otecki at ASP Wroclaw**) and sell canvases. Present that as inference.
41. **Murals die of demolition, not paint failure.** Outdoor sacrificial anti-graffiti coatings
    last about **one year**. **"Odzyski," Lodz, 2014**, by Cekas, Chazme, Proembrion, Sepe and
    Tone, called one of Europe's largest, is gone -- the project page now reads **"Mural nie
    istnieje."**
42. **Scene anchors.** **Fundacja Urban Forms**, Lodz, registered **31 October 2008**, first
    murals **2010**, at least **149 murals**, digital museum launched **29 December 2024**
    digitising **over 150**. **Gdansk Zaspa:** 10 murals in **1997** under **Rafal Roskowinski**;
    Monumental Art Festival **2009-2016**; **58 large-format murals**. **Natalia Rak's** girl with
    the watering can, Bialystok, **2013**, for **Folk on the Street**; on a Polish stamp in 2014.
43. **The legal line.** The **ustawa krajobrazowa** (24 April 2015, in force 11 September 2015)
    defines advertising as promotion "in any visual form." **An art mural carries no promotional
    message and falls outside it; a mural with a logo does not.** Permissions stack: owner or
    *wspolnota mieszkaniowa*, city, *konserwator zabytkow* if listed, local *uchwala krajobrazowa*.

### The science, which collapsed

44. **Jarosz, Colflesh and Wiley (2012), "Uncorking the muse,"** *Consciousness and Cognition*
    21(1) 487-493, published as a **Short Communication**. **40 male social drinkers**, 20 per
    cell, recruited **via Craigslist**, dosed on **100-proof Smirnoff at 0.88 g/kg** with bagels
    and the film **Ratatouille**, **BAC 0.075**, tested on the **Remote Associates Test, 15 items,
    one minute each**. Result: **0.58 correct versus 0.42**, p = .001, d = 1.08.
45. **What the coverage omits: no placebo, no blinding.** And the famous "sudden insight" finding
    was **marginal, p < .08**; only a narrower count reached p = .039.
46. **It did not replicate.** **Benedek and Zohrer (2020), "Creativity on tap 2"** -- preregistered,
    randomized, **double-blind, placebo-controlled**, **N = 125**, three groups (placebo, BAC 0.03,
    BAC 0.06). **RAT: no effect. F(2,122) = 0.02, p = .98.** No effect on divergent thinking. **No
    working-memory impairment either**, contradicting Jarosz. Funded by a grant from the **Dutch
    Beer Institute** -- a beer-funded study reporting nulls is not biased in the obvious direction.
47. **The instrument is noisy:** RAT internal consistency **.45 and .46**, retest r = .32, and it
    was low in Jarosz too. A very noisy test producing very large published effects.
48. **The most damaging result: Lapp, Collins and Izzo (1994)**, *American Journal of Psychology*
    107, 173-206. **Balanced placebo design**, crossing real alcohol with told-alcohol, on
    **arranging wildflower pictures by colour, shape and number** -- **the only alcohol study ever
    run on a visual creative task.** **No pharmacological effect. Novelty rose when subjects
    thought they had drunk, whether or not they had.**
49. **There is no research at all on alcohol and visual artists.** A systematic Europe PMC search
    returned **zero experimental studies**. Everything in the literature is verbal.
50. **The 17 painters ever asked said don't.** Roe (1946), via Ludwig (1990): 17 established
    artists, all drank, **"all but one avoided drinking while painting,"** and none used alcohol
    to overcome technical difficulties. Ludwig: **alcohol "provided the freedom for painting but
    impaired the discipline."**
51. **Long-run output: Ludwig (1990)**, 34 heavy-drinking creative achievers (28 writers, only
    **2 artists**): **alcohol directly decreased output in 26 of 34, 76.5 percent**; directly
    increased it in **3, 8.8 percent**; indirect benefits clustered in the **early** phases.
    "No simplistic conclusions are possible."
52. **The self-undermining argument -- ours, not published.** Jarosz's own proposed mechanism,
    broadened associative spread, is exactly what the sleep literature attributes to **REM** (Cai
    2009: REM specifically improved RAT scores; Wagner 2004: sleepers were **more than twice as
    likely** to find a hidden shortcut). And Ebrahim et al. 2013: **"The onset of the first REM
    sleep period is significantly delayed at all doses."** The drink buys an hour of what the
    night gives free -- and delays the night. **No study has tested this. Mark it as inference.**
53. **"Write drunk, edit sober" is Peter De Vries, *Reuben, Reuben*, 1964, p. 242**, spoken by a
    fictional dissolute poet, **Gowan McGland**, and the original is symmetrical. First pinned on
    Hemingway in a **2005 Usenet post to rec.woodworking**.

## PART C -- VOCABULARY, VERIFIED ONLY

*na zdrowie* -- "to health," with **two dictionary senses**: what you say to someone who has
sneezed, and what you say before a toast. *sto lat* -- a wish and a song for birthdays and
name-days, **not** a clinking toast; its author and origin are unknown. *przy piwie* -- "over a
beer," the standard frame for an honest political conversation. *do dna* -- to the bottom; the
practice is attested, the phrase is not a dictionary headword. Verified rhyme: *"A kto nie wypije,
tego we dwa kije."* *strzemienny*, offered as *"Strzemiennego!"* -- the last round before the group
disperses, from *strzemie*, stirrup; its glass was a *kusztyk* or *kulawka*, **with no base, so it
cannot be set down until it is empty** (*kulawka* = "lame one"). The modern living word is
*rozchodniak / rozchodniaczek*. Feasts closed on *"Kochajmy sie!"*, also the last line of
*Pan Tadeusz*.

Measures: *setka* / *seta* = 100 ml; *cwiartka* = 250 ml but **era-specific**, withdrawn after EU
accession; *polowka* = a half-litre; *flaszka* = a bottle, no fixed volume; *malpka* = 100-200 ml,
producing what the linguist Katarzyna Klosinska calls *malpi rozum*, monkey-brains; *krowa* = 0.7 l;
*szpilka* and *baczek* = the slim and round 0.33 l beer bottles; *kieliszek* -- the dictionary gives
no volume, and the one Polish guide fetched says **35 to 40 ml**.

Hangover: *kac*, from German *Katzenjammer*; *na kacu*; **kac moralny**, a moral hangover, not
necessarily drink-related -- a whole country can have one. *klin klinem* (Latin *clavum clavo
pellere*) is general-purpose, not only alcoholic; *klin* alone is the morning drink. **Kefir is the
sober man's cure; the klin is the drinker's.**

Places: *monopolowy*, named after the 1924 state monopoly; *melina*, a flat trading alcohol
illegally; *meta* -- **a place**, a den or hideout, and in modern slang methamphetamine; *knajpa*,
*piwiarnia*, *karczma*, *zajazd*. **bar mleczny -- the milk bar is by law alcohol-free**; PRL
texture: chipped thick ceramic with blue rims, cast-aluminium cutlery, laminate tables, welded steel
chairs, prices to the single *grosz*. *pogotowiarze* -- the night couriers Warsaw's ban produced,
11-25 zloty for a delivered beer. Drunk-register ladder, pitying to hostile: *pijaczek*, *pijak*,
*menel*, *zul*; the real synonym chain includes *moczymorda*, soak-face.

Sessions: *impreza* (neutral), *melanz* (1990s youth slang, drugs in the definition), *libacja*
(formal, disapproving -- what a police report says), *biba*, *popijawa*. Verbs: *chlac / chlanie*,
labelled colloquial **and** vulgar, Szczerek's word; *nawalic sie*, to get plastered.

Drinks: *wodka czysta* versus *gatunkowa* is the axis a Pole argues along. *bimber* is the native
word, *samogon* the Russian loan; **home distilling for personal use is a criminal offence in
Poland** (2004 Supreme Court ruling, up to three years), and bimber's etymology is genuinely
unknown -- write it unresolved. *jabol*, cheap sweet fortified fruit wine; *spirytus*, 95 percent,
legally sold; *nalewka*, the respectable home infusion. **Wyborowa** became, in **1927**, the world's
first registered vodka trademark. **Zubrowka** is *gatunkowa*, and its blade of grass is **purely
decorative**. **Soplica** takes its name and its manor from *Pan Tadeusz*.

Food: *zakaska*, from *kasac*, to bite -- the bite that follows the swallow; *zagryzka* is homelier
and explicitly alcohol-linked, *zagrycha* is street. **Use *ogorek kiszony*, brine-fermented and
sour, never *ogorek konserwowy*, which is vinegar-pickled.** The verified pairing is with vodka and
*sledz w oleju z cebula*.

Szczerek's coinages, all verified: *Polska A / Polska B*, *Europa B*, *polperyferium*, *peryferium
do kwadratu*, *Pustac Wewnatrzobwarzankowa* (the Inner-Bagel Wasteland = the middle of Poland),

drinking culture is **sarmatyzm**.

# ADDENDUM -- POLAND'S OWN ALCOHOL HISTORY

*(The Schrad dossier was still being written when this brief was drafted; its Poland section
landed afterwards. Everything below supersedes the earlier caution at fact 31 that the propinacja
was unverified. It is now verified -- but from non-Schrad sources, and the tiers differ.)*

**54. Propinacja is real, dated and defined.** Polish Wikipedia: the **exclusive right of the
owner of landed property to produce and sell beer, gorzalka and mead within his estate**, plus
the privilege of importing them, with sale taking place in **the lord's karczma, where the sale
of outside alcohol was forbidden**. **Introduced by a Sejm statute in 1496**, widespread by the
17th century. The privilege was often leased out -- to Jews, village heads or innkeepers -- and
**the owner's income from propinacja often exceeded his income from every other activity.**

**55. The compulsion, in three documented forms.** Peasants could not buy alcohol not produced by
their lord; or could produce their own but had to pay by volume; and **had to buy at least a set
quota of vodka or okowita -- with the unbought remainder dumped in front of their houses and
charged to them.** Polish Wikipedia records the consequence: **people in the countryside avoided
holding weddings for fear of the excessive vodka quota the estate owner imposed on them.**
(English Wikipedia's article is flagged as under-cited; the Polish one is better. Both are
starting points, not citations.)

**56. The epigram, VERBATIM (Karl Grolmann, via Polish Wikipedia):** *"czego nie wydarto chlopu
daninami, panszczyzna, odbierano posrednio przez wodke"* -- **"What was not torn from the peasant
by dues and corvee was taken indirectly through vodka."** This is the best single sentence in the
whole evidence base for Chapter 3.

**57. Abolition dates, and they are three different dates because there were three partitions:**
**Prussian partition 1845, Galicia 1889, Russian partition 1898** -- with a note that Galician
propination effectively persisted, and a 1905 law scheduled its end for **1911**. The partition
map is also the alcohol-law map, which is exactly Szczerek's Polska A / Polska B point.

**58. Galicia, LOW TIER -- flag it or leave it.** A heritage-tourism blog drawing on Ukrainian
scholarship gives **23,269 taverns in Galicia at the end of the 19th century, one per 233 people,
26 litres of vodka per person per year**, against **8 percent literacy**. **Do not print these as
fact without independent verification.** The same source's structural claims are safer and match
Schrad's Russian pattern: coerced quotas, and **indigenous mead and beer traditions degraded in
favour of cheap lord-supplied potato distillate.**

**59. The hazard the chapter must handle.** The propinacja was leased heavily to Jewish
*arendarze*, and the antisemitic fable that "calculated Jewish sobriety" enabled a predatory
monopoly over Christian peasants was propagated for centuries, **particularly by
temperance-preaching Catholic clergy** (Nadler, reviewing **Glenn Dynner, *Yankel's Tavern: Jews,
Liquor, and Life in the Kingdom of Poland*, OUP 2014**). Dynner demolishes both that fable and its
mirror image, documenting substantial Jewish alcoholism from about **5,000 kvitlekh** in the YIVO
archives, and shows the tavern as a site of Polish-Jewish intermingling. **The book must name the
lessee system without reproducing the fable, and should say plainly that clerical temperance and
antisemitic scapegoating were historically entangled.**

**60. Poland did revolt against the supply, and it worked, and it was crushed.** The
**Brotherhood of Sobriety (Bractwo Trzezwosci)** originated **in 1844 in Silesia** -- i.e. with
**Fr Jan Alojzy Ficek** in the Prussian partition -- and had **papal approval from Pius IX in
1851**. In the Russian partition from September 1858, **three quarters of the population of
Grodno, Kaunas and Vilnius took the pledge**; sales fell **33 percent in Grodno, 40 in Vilnius,
70 in Kaunas**. Schrad, VERBATIM: **"The finance ministry demanded that the Catholics renounce
their temperance heresy and preach as the Orthodox did: that vodka was a 'harmless' and even
'necessary' indulgence."** Repression: taverns attacked, army deployed, protesters flogged and
**force-fed liquor through funnels**, **over 780 tried before military tribunals and exiled to
Siberia**; **temperance societies outlawed in 1863**, the year of the January Uprising (that
connection is the book's own inference, not a sourced claim). A British journalist, VERBATIM:
**"The teetotalers were flogged into drinking... These things sound incredible, but they are
true."** **NOTE: Schrad attributes the crackdown to Tsar Nicholas I, who died in 1855. In 1859
the tsar was Alexander II. Do not repeat his error.**

**61. Solidarity put alcohol on its banners -- and this is the Polish version of Schrad's thesis,
written by a Pole.** Jacek Moskalewicz, *Journal of Health Inequalities* 2019, VERBATIM: **"The
Solidarity movement already had on its banners the alcohol question in the early 1980s. The
working class apparently blamed the government for pushing alcohol to manipulate drunken society
and to discredit its demands for a better life and freedom."** Out of those negotiations came the
**Act of 26 October 1982 on upbringing in sobriety and counteracting alcoholism** -- the law still
in force today, and the one that bans alcohol advertising except beer. By the end of the decade
**consumption had levelled off at about six litres of ethanol per capita.**

**62. Then 1989 dismantled it.** Moskalewicz, VERBATIM: **"the control system was totally rejected
as a remnant of socialism, and most restrictions were lifted."** Licensing was decentralised to
local councils, whose budgets were fed by the licence fees, so **local stakeholders had an
interest in more outlets: their number rose from about 40,000 in the late 1980s to more than
200,000 a few years later.** The brewing industry expanded capacity, **doubling and then tripling
consumption in under ten years**, and **drinking among 15-16-year-olds doubled**. Then the
**2002 spirits excise cut of 30 percent**: Zatonski, Janik-Koncewicz and Zatonski (2022) find
Poland's long health improvement **halted in 2002**, with alcohol-attributable mortality rising
from **7.5 to 17.8 per 100,000 between 2002 and 2019** and **130,000 cumulative deaths from
100 percent alcohol-attributable causes, 2002-2019** (2019 alone: 10,396). Rehm et al., *Lancet
Regional Health -- Europe*, 2023: Polish alcohol control was **"mostly dismantled."** And when a
spirits excise rise was announced in **2013**, the industry won the framing on grey-market and
tax-revenue arguments and **the public health community failed to make health a significant
element of the debate** (Zatonski, Hawkins and McKee, 2018).

**63. Schrad's one paragraph on modern Poland, and the correction it needs.** VERBATIM: **"When
communism collapsed in Poland, sixty-one percent of alcohol consumed was in the form of distilled
spirits. By 2002, it was down to twenty-six percent... male life expectancy in Poland jumped four
full years"** -- against Russia, where vodka's share rose from 66 to 71 percent and male life
expectancy fell five years. The numbers for 1989-2002 are real. But **he stopped the clock in
2002, the exact year Poland cut spirits excise by 30 percent and reversed course**, and he
attributed the shift to a deliberate migration strategy the Polish literature does not support --
Moskalewicz credits a deregulated brewing industry. **If the book uses Schrad's
Poland-versus-Russia comparison, it must carry this correction.**
