# OUTLINE

## TITLE

# RAKSZAWA, WISCONSIN
### Why Does Bober Drink Beer? -- Six Centuries, One Wall, and a Can That Lies About Where It Comes From

(Rakszawa, Podkarpackie, is the Polish village where the Pabst Blue Ribbon in Bober's hand was
actually brewed. The title is the joke and the thesis in two words.)

## THESIS, ONE SENTENCE

Bober's beer is not a chemical, a weakness or a national trait -- it is a Polish product in an
American costume, standing in for six centuries of engineered supply and one hour a day that
nobody will pay for, and the only thing it reliably delivers is permission to stop.

## SPINE

Ch 1 asks the question properly. Ch 2 destroys the lazy answer (national character). Ch 3
supplies the real supply-side answer (states built it). Ch 4 destroys the flattering answer
(the beer is a costume, the muse is a failed replication). Ch 5 supplies the answer none of the
sources reached.

---

## CHAPTER 1 -- "The Light Goes Flat at Half Past Three"

**Core argument.** The first beer of Bober's day arrives at a precise, non-arbitrary moment --
when the light flattens and he can no longer judge colour -- and that timing means the question
"why does Bober drink beer?" is a question about work, not about weakness.

**Must use.**
- The character-bible constraint, stated as fact and defended: **it is not the morning**. The
  first one lands mid-afternoon, when the light goes flat.
- The paint's own limits, which make the day a physics problem before it is a moral one: acrylic
  facade paint needs **plus 10 to plus 30 C**, silicone works to **plus 5 C**; no rain, wind,
  frost, high humidity or harsh direct sun; **the Polish exterior season is six to seven months**.
- **A 1,000 m2 gable needs 170 to 330 litres of base paint** for two coats. Scale is the point.
- The lift: **podnosnik koszowy**, and the **operator must hold UDT certification**. Scaffolding
  is rejected on tall buildings as uneconomic.
- Duration, so the reader feels the shape of the job: **50-100 m2 is 4-10 days**; the 24-storey
  Lodz Witcher wall took **eight painters over two weeks**; **Inti's 11-storey wall took over 11
  days solo**.
- **przy piwie** -- "over a beer," the standard Polish frame for where an honest conversation
  happens. Introduce the vocabulary discipline here.
- Q9 (Szczerek, "in every normal country...") as the book's licence to take a stupid-sounding
  question seriously.

**Previous chapter established:** nothing; this is the door.
**Next chapter argues:** that the obvious answer -- he is Polish, Poles drink -- is factually
false, and the numbers say so.
**Opening move.** Start on the lift at 15:40 with the can unopened and the wall two-thirds
finished, and refuse the anecdote: give the reader the temperature, the litres, the certificate
number the operator has to hold. Then open the can. The comedy is the mismatch between the
seriousness of the apparatus and the ordinariness of the object.

## CHAPTER 2 -- "Eighth Place, and Falling"

**Core argument.** The stereotype is not an exaggeration of a true thing, it is a wrong thing:
Poland ranks **8th** in Europe, is **falling**, and has been quietly turning into a beer country
-- so national character explains nothing and the explanation has to be found elsewhere.

**Must use.**
- The WHO 2024 table: **Romania 17.07, Latvia 14.18, Portugal 13.75, Czechia 13.52, Bulgaria
  13.25, Spain 12.07, Austria 11.60, POLAND 11.51**, then Hungary, Lithuania, France, and
  **Germany at 10.93**. Czechia, not Poland, is Central Europe's champion.
- The direction of travel: **2019 was the modern peak at 10.99**, 2024 is **9.79**; 2013 to 2023
  is **10.81 to 10.00**.
- The correction that keeps the chapter honest: **1981 was higher at 11.21**, and **the 1990 GUS
  figure does not exist** -- the domestic series begins in 1992.
- The two-figure discipline, stated openly to the reader: **GUS/KCPU 8.8 (whole population,
  registered sales) versus WHO 11.51 (15+, plus unrecorded)**. Never mixed.
- The vodka-to-beer shift as the real story: spirits **62.0 percent of intake in 1990**, **30.8
  in 2000**, **40.8 in 2024**; beer **24.8 to 51.5**; beer in natural litres **38.6 (1992) to
  100.5 (2018, the peak) to about 85.8 (2024)**.
- The one statistic that survives the debunking and hurts: **79,000 alcoholic cirrhosis deaths,
  1999 to 2022**, with men aged 45-64 going **436 to 2,583**. This is the book's single honest
  acknowledgement that it could end badly. Use it once, do not moralise, move on.
- Q14 (Wisniewski on gonzo: "it's never the truth") to make the stereotype, not Poland, the butt.

**Previous chapter established:** the beer has a place in a working day with hard physical rules.
**Next chapter argues:** if it is not national character, then it is history -- somebody built
the supply.
**Opening move.** Open with the joke everyone makes about Poles and vodka, then put the WHO table
on the page and let the reader watch the joke lose to Romania by five and a half litres and to
Czechia by two. Then the twist: Bober is not even drinking vodka.

## CHAPTER 3 -- "The Shop That Opened at Ten and Could Sell Nothing Until One"

**Core argument.** Nobody in Poland chose this supply: landlords compelled it, states monopolised
it, the PRL rationed it, and a modern parliament made it cheaper -- the reflex was engineered
from the outside, and it is still being engineered now.

**Must use.**
- **Przymus propinacyjny**, the feudal obligation on peasants to buy the lord's alcohol, and
  **przymus konsumpcyjny**, set annual quantities. Polish drinking has a **coercive economic
  skeleton, not merely a convivial one.** The **karczma** rivalled the Church as village
  institution.
- **Panstwowy Monopol Spirytusowy, 1924** -- the reason a liquor shop is a *monopolowy*.
- **The 1980s shop that opened at 10:00 and was forbidden to sell before 13:00.**
- **Alcohol ration cards from martial law, 13 December 1981**, in a system "considerably wider
  than under the German occupation" -- and the mechanic outsiders get wrong: **the cards only
  entitled you to buy; you still paid with money.**
- The Russian case, **explicitly labelled Russian**, as the clean statement of the mechanism:
  the **kabak** imported from Kazan in **1552**, the **1649** code that bound the peasant and the
  vodka trade in the same law, the monopoly at **over a third of imperial revenue** and
  **100 percent of the army's budget**. Q6 ("not because the Russian people demanded it, but
  because the Russian state supplied it"), Q7 ("immediate bankruptcy"), Q8 (Kokorev: "an
  exemption from the rules"). **State clearly that Schrad never wrote about Poland and that the
  parallel is the book's own.**
- The Soviet **December 1958** decree that abolished the single glass and thereby invented the
  three-man bottle: **2 roubles 87 kopecks, one rouble each, 13 kopecks over, poured "po
  bulkam"** -- and the custom outliving its own arithmetic after 1970. Describe the Polish
  version of the act; **do not name a Polish idiom for it.**
- The modern state doing the same job: **the 2002 excise cut of 30 percent** and spirits doubling
  from 1.7 to 3.4 litres pure per head by 2008; **32 bottles per minimum wage in 2001 versus 107
  in 2021**; **119,598 outlets, one per 302 residents against one pharmacy per 2,700**.
- And the state now reversing: **Krakow's ban since 1 July 2023** (interventions down 48.5 then
  61.8 percent), **Warsaw city-wide from 1 June 2026**, **excise up 5 percent not 15** after
  Nawrocki's veto (Q20), the advertising ban **not yet law** -- and the collision worth naming,
  that such a ban would make an alcohol-brand mural illegal.

**Previous chapter established:** the stereotype is quantitatively false, so the cause is not in
the people.
**Next chapter argues:** if the state supplied the drink, the market supplied the meaning -- and
the meaning is a costume.
**Opening move.** Open on the shopfront: 10 a.m., lights on, staff in, and three hours in which
a shop that sells only alcohol may sell nothing at all. Then walk backwards from it to the
karczma and forwards from it to 119,598 outlets.

## CHAPTER 4 -- "A Prize Nobody Won, A Muse Nobody Found"

**Core argument.** Both of the flattering stories on the can are false in the same way: the blue
ribbon commemorates a prize that was never awarded, and the science that says drinking makes you
creative did not survive replication -- what is left in both cases is belief doing the work that
was credited to the object.

**Must use.**
- The corrected sentence, every clause sourced: **the ribbon was on the bottle from 1882**; the
  **1893 fair gave identical bronze medals to every beer scoring 80 percent or more**, with **no
  gold, no silver, no grand prize and no ribbons**; and **the slogan was written in the 1950s**.
- The anti-brand as a brand: Kalmanovitz's **advertising cut to zero in 1985**, then Portland in
  **2002**, **first growth since 1978**, **plus 15 percent in 2003**, gallery openings and band
  ads in **25 markets** that never mentioned the beer. Q1 ("the minute you advertise on TV, I'm
  done with you") and Q2 ("we let them decide for themselves").
- **The Art Can Contest**: **300 million cans**, **100,000 dollars**, and **Ashley Dreyfus, a
  mixed-media artist**, on **30 million-plus cans**. Pabst literally pays people who do Bober's
  job.
- **The inversion.** The can in his hand was brewed at **Rakszawa** by **Van Pur**, the largest
  independent Polish brewer, whose own boast is that Rakszawa's beer "is exported to highly
  demanding and distant foreign markets" (Q5); it launched **12 May 2020** (Q3), cost **about 4
  zloty in Polomarket**, and the Polish beer press gave it **2 out of 10** (Q4) -- while the same
  label is **8.45 euro a litre in Germany**, **"USA Trash Lager" in York**, and a **44-dollar
  barrel-aged ale served with a champagne flute in China**. Flag the 2026 availability as
  unverified.
- Then the science, in the same shape. **Jarosz 2012: 40 men, Craigslist, Ratatouille, BAC 0.075,
  one word-association test, no placebo**, and the insight finding **only marginal at p < .08**.
  **Benedek and Zohrer 2020: preregistered, double-blind, N = 125, p = .98** (Q17), on a test with
  **reliability of .45**, funded by the **Dutch Beer Institute**.
- **Lapp 1994** (Q18): the **only alcohol study ever run on a visual creative task** found **no
  drug effect**, and novelty rose **when subjects merely believed they had drunk**.
- **There is no research at all on alcohol and visual artists.** And **the 17 painters ever asked
  said don't** (Q19): freedom yes, discipline destroyed.
- **De Vries, not Hemingway** (Q15, Q16), including the amputated second half of the sentence.

**Previous chapter established:** the state supplied the drink.
**Next chapter argues:** if neither the label nor the ethanol is doing the work, something else
is -- and it is on the lift, not in the can.
**Opening move.** Open on the can itself, held at arm's length at forty feet, and read it out
loud as a document: the ribbon, the date, the slogan, the barcode of a Podkarpackie brewery.
Every claim on it is either a lie or a foreign address.

## CHAPTER 5 -- "Ten Minutes Nobody Pays For"

**Core argument -- the book's original contribution.** What the beer actually buys is not
inspiration but **an authorised stop**: mural work is priced by the square metre and pays nothing
for thinking, every other constraint in the day is external and non-negotiable, and the can is
the only object on the lift that supplies a legitimate reason to stand still and look at the
wall. The muse was always belief, as Lapp showed -- but the belief that operates is not "this
will make me better," it is "I am allowed to not be working right now."

**Where it comes from -- the friction, not a summary.**
- The economics section says the trade is **paid per square metre (200 to 1,000 PLN/m2)** and
  that **design and permit time is unpaid and can run months**. Nobody in the file connects that
  to the creativity literature. The book does: **the trade prices surface, not thought**, so the
  thinking has to be smuggled in under another heading, and the beer is the heading.
- The creativity literature says the effect is **expectancy, not ethanol** (Lapp), and that
  the one associative mechanism that really does work is **REM sleep**, which alcohol **delays at
  all doses** (Ebrahim 2013; Cai 2009; Wagner 2004). **Mark this trade-off as inference; no study
  has tested it.** So the beer is a worse version of a free thing -- which only makes sense if
  what it is really buying is not the associative state but the pause.
- Szczerek supplies the structure of the ending, and it must not be a redemption: in *Mordor* the
  drink is revealed to be a joke on the drinker (Q12, the Vigor label and "Here. Drink up.") --
  **and he keeps drinking.** Knowing the ribbon is fake changes nothing about half past three.
- Szczerek also supplies the refusal of shame (Q13): "It may be ugly but it's ours." Applied to
  the can: a Polish beer in an American costume is not a defeat, it is **the most local possible
  object wearing the most foreign possible label** -- which is exactly what a Polish mural
  festival, a Polish supermarket and a Polish artist all are.
- The mortality of the object closes it: **"Odzyski," 2014, one of the largest murals in Europe
  -- "Mural nie istnieje."** Anti-graffiti coating lasts about **a year** outdoors; walls die of
  demolition and thermomodernisation, not of paint. **The wall is temporary, the pause is not
  billable, and the only thing on the lift that is finished is the can.**

**Must use:** the per-square-metre rates and the unpaid design time; Lapp's expectancy result;
the REM delay at all doses (flagged as inference); Q12; Q13; "Mural nie istnieje"; the six-to-
seven-month season; and one sentence of Ludwig -- **76.5 percent had their output directly
decreased**, "no simplistic conclusions are possible."

**Previous chapter established:** the label lies and the science collapsed.
**Next chapter argues:** there is none. End on the lift.
**Opening move.** Start with the invoice. A mural is billed in square metres, and there is no
line on it for looking. Then go up forty feet and spend the chapter inside the ten minutes the
invoice does not mention. End with the wall two-thirds finished, the light going, and the one
object in the frame that is complete.

## CHAPTER 3 -- "What the Dues Did Not Take, the Vodka Took"

**Core argument, sharpened.** Poland did not drift into this: for four centuries a landlord had
the legal right to make the alcohol, the legal monopoly on selling it, and the power to compel a
quota of it -- and every regime since, tsarist, communist and democratic, has rebuilt some version
of that machine, including the one in office now.

**Must use (replacing the earlier list, keeping its second half).**
- **Propinacja, defined and dated: a Sejm statute of 1496**, widespread by the 17th century -- the
  exclusive right to produce and sell beer, gorzalka and mead on the estate, sold in **the lord's
  karczma where outside alcohol was banned**, with income from it often exceeding income from
  everything else.
- **The compulsion:** a set quota of vodka each household had to buy, with **the unbought
  remainder dumped in front of the house and charged for**; and people avoiding weddings in the
  village rather than trigger the quota.
- **The epigram, and it should probably be the chapter's spine: "What was not torn from the
  peasant by dues and corvee was taken indirectly through vodka."** (Karl Grolmann.)
- **Three abolition dates because there were three partitions: Prussian 1845, Galicia 1889,
  Russian 1898.** Then land it: the partition map is still the map of **Polska A and Polska B**.
- **The antisemitism hazard, handled head-on.** The propinacja was widely leased to Jewish
  *arendarze*, and the fable that Jewish sobriety enabled a predatory monopoly was spread for
  centuries, **especially by temperance-preaching Catholic clergy**. Cite **Dynner, *Yankel's
  Tavern* (OUP 2014)**, who demolishes it from inside the archive. Name the fable, name who spread
  it, refuse it. Do not skip this to keep the chapter tidy.
- **Poland tried to quit, and the state made it stop.** The **Brotherhood of Sobriety, from 1844 in
  Silesia under Fr Ficek**, papal approval 1851; **three quarters of Grodno, Kaunas and Vilnius
  took the pledge**; sales fell **33, 40 and 70 percent**; the finance ministry demanded the
  Catholic clergy **preach that vodka was "harmless" and even "necessary"**; **over 780 tried by
  military tribunal**, flogged, **force-fed liquor through funnels**, exiled; **temperance
  societies outlawed in 1863**. And the British journalist's line: **"These things sound
  incredible, but they are true."** Do not repeat Schrad's "Nicholas I."
- **The PRL beats:** the **1980s shop open at 10:00 and forbidden to sell before 13:00**;
  **alcohol ration cards from martial law, 13 December 1981**, cards entitling but not paying.
- **Solidarity, which is the Polish Schrad and is not by Schrad.** Moskalewicz: **"The working
  class apparently blamed the government for pushing alcohol to manipulate drunken society and to
  discredit its demands for a better life and freedom"** -- producing the **Act of 26 October
  1982**, still the governing law, and consumption levelling at about six litres.
- **Then the reversal, which is the chapter's punchline.** After 1989 the control system was
  **"totally rejected as a remnant of socialism"**; councils were paid by licence fees, so
  **outlets went from about 40,000 to over 200,000**; the brewers doubled then tripled consumption
  in under a decade. Then the **2002 excise cut of 30 percent**, after which Poland's long health
  improvement **halted**, alcohol-attributable mortality went **7.5 to 17.8 per 100,000**, and
  **130,000 people died of purely alcohol-attributable causes between 2002 and 2019**. By 2023 the
  international verdict was that Polish alcohol control had been **"mostly dismantled."**
- **Keep from the original list:** the Russian material as the clean statement of the mechanism
  (kabak 1552, code of 1649, a third of imperial revenue, the whole army budget, Q6, Q7, Q8), the
  **December 1958** decree that invented the three-man bottle, **119,598 outlets**, **32 versus
  107 bottles per minimum wage**, and the present-day reversal (Krakow, Warsaw, the veto, the
  advertising bill that is not law).
- **Carry the correction to Schrad in the text, not a footnote:** his flattering Poland paragraph
  stops the clock in 2002, the exact year Poland cut the tax and turned around.

**Opening move (revised).** Open in 1496 with one sentence of statute, then jump to a 1980s
shopfront that may not sell for three hours, then to a 2002 tax cut and a mortality curve that
bends the wrong way in the same year. Three regimes, one habit of building the supply and then
being surprised by it.
