# Lukasz Bober -- Character Bible

The protagonist. Everything downstream is written around this man. Two tiers:
CANON is given and cannot be contradicted. INVENTED is the book's working
license -- consistent across chapters, but changeable if the author says so.

---

## CANON (supplied by the author, non-negotiable)

- Name: Lukasz Bober. Polish. Called Bober.
- Drinks Pabst Blue Ribbon. Specifically PBR, not a Polish lager, not craft.
- Artist. Mixed media.
- Musician.
- Muralist. Paints massive walls into colorful life.
- Does not give a fuck.
- Loves books.
- Loves boobs.
- And the odd thinking time.

That is the whole of the canon. Eight facts. The book must not add a
biography that overwrites them -- it must EXPLAIN them.

---

## THE CENTRAL PUZZLE (the book's engine)

A Polish artist, in Poland, drinking an American beer that exists to signal
that you are not trying. The question "why does Bober drink beer?" is not
rhetorical and not a joke setup. It has at least six real answers, and the
book's structure is the peeling of them:

1. Because it is wet and it is there. (The stupid answer. Also true.)
2. Because his hands need to stop shaking and his eye needs to stop judging
   at hour nine on a wall. (The physiological answer.)
3. Because a Pole drinking is doing a social ritual with rules older than
   the state. (Fox's answer.)
4. Because six centuries of landlords, partitions, and states engineered
   the supply and taught the reflex. (Schrad's answer.)
5. Because PBR is a costume -- a chosen class signal, a flag planted
   against the Polish vodka inheritance AND against craft-beer bourgeois
   seriousness. (Szczerek's answer, the identity answer.)
6. Because the beer is the only part of his day that asks nothing of him.
   (The one that hurts.)

The comedy comes from taking the question far too seriously. The book is
funny the way a very smart drunk friend is funny at 2am -- rigorous,
digressive, occasionally devastating.

## INVENTED TEXTURE (consistent across chapters, the book's license)

Physical: big hands, permanently paint-cuffed. Forearms. Paint under the
nails that no solvent gets out, so he has stopped trying. Wears the same
three t-shirts, all of them ruined and all of them kept.

Working method: cans and rollers and whatever else -- house paint, wheat
paste, offcuts, sanding back what he did yesterday. Mixed media means he
will glue something to a wall if the wall wants it. Works up on a boom lift
or scaffolding, weather permitting, in a country where weather rarely
permits.

The beer's place in the day: not the morning. The book must be precise
about this and it matters -- Bober is not a man drinking to get through the
morning. The first one lands when the light goes flat and he can no longer
judge color, mid-afternoon. That is a real thing that happens to people who
paint outdoors, and it is the book's best single detail.

Music: he plays. Badly enough to enjoy it, well enough to be asked. The
band is the social object; the band is also four men with a reason to be in
a room with beer.

Books: he actually reads them. This is the trait that breaks the
stereotype and the book should use it as its hinge. A man who reads is not
a man who is merely numbing.

Boobs: he is bawdy, unreconstructed, funny about it, and not a creep. The
book handles this the way a good comic novel does -- as appetite, as part of
a man who says what he thinks, never as leer. If a joke would embarrass the
woman in the room rather than Bober, it is the wrong joke.

The thinking time: rare, unannounced, and the whole point. Ten minutes on a
lift forty feet up with a beer, looking at a wall that is two-thirds
finished, thinking about nothing anybody can transcribe. The book ends
somewhere near here.

## TONE RULES

- Comic register, HBR spine. Every chapter still makes ONE argument and
  proves it with real evidence.
- Bober is never the butt. Poland is never the butt. The stereotype is the
  butt.
- No pity, no diagnosis, no intervention arc. This is not a recovery
  memoir and must never smell like one.
- The book is allowed to notice that this could end badly. Once. Honestly.
  Then it moves on, because Bober would.
- Verified facts only. Where a fact is unverified, the prose does not use
  it. Polish vocabulary must be correct or absent.
