## 1.2 THE BLUE RIBBON: **THIS IS A MARKETING MYTH.** Handle with care.

**What the company implies:** PBR won a blue ribbon (or gold medal, or "grand prize") for best beer in America at the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago, and renamed itself in honour of that win.

**What is actually true, in ascending order of certainty:**

**(a) The blue ribbon predates the fair by eleven years.** Pabst tied real blue silk ribbons around bottle necks starting in **1882**. The Pabst Mansion's own historical account states the company consumed **1 million feet of ribbon per year** doing it. When packaging shifted to cans, the physical ribbon became impractical and the image of a ribbon was printed instead. The ribbon was never a prize — it was a bottle decoration, invented for retail differentiation.
https://www.pabstmansion.com/2022/03/americasbest/
https://www.smithsonianmag.com/arts-culture/where-did-pabst-win-that-blue-ribbon-138975181/

**(b) The 1893 fair did not award blue ribbons, and did not rank winners.** The most rigorous debunking is by Dr. Loih (drloihjournal), working from the 1891 exposition contest rules and Hubert Howe Bancroft's *The Book of the Fair* (1893). Findings:
- Judges scored entries against a fixed standard of excellence, **not head-to-head**.
- **Every** beer scoring 80% or higher received an **identical bronze St. Gaudens medal** plus a parchment certificate.
- **No gold or silver medals were awarded. No grand prize existed. No ribbons of any colour were awarded.**
- The beer judges later deviated from the official rules and invented their own scoring categories — the origin of the confusion that multiple exhibitors then exploited.
- Pabst received **the same bronze medal as every other qualifying brewer**, and marketed itself as the grand-prize winner.
- Both the Pabst Mansion and the Wisconsin Historical Society **revised their websites** after this research.
https://drloihjournal.blogspot.com/2017/10/pabst-brewing-company-false-claim-1893-worlds-fair.html

**(c) Wikipedia's own hedge:** "Whether the brand actually won an award in 1893 is unclear."
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pabst_Blue_Ribbon

**(d) Sources that still repeat the win.** *Smithsonian Magazine* states "Pabst's Best Select won the top beer award at the 1893 Exposition" without explaining the fair's award system. The Pabst Mansion account describes a two-horse race against Budweiser, a judging controversy over alleged "chemical impurities" in Budweiser, a judge switching his vote to Pabst, and an official announcement on **Wednesday, 15 November 1893**. **These accounts conflict with the primary-source evidence in (b) and should be presented in the book as the myth's own internal literature, not as fact.**

**(e) The slogan is a 1950s invention.** The line **"Selected as America's Best in 1893"** — still on packaging today — was added in **the 1950s** by a struggling Pabst to drum up sales. It is not contemporaneous with the award. Six decades separate the event from the claim about the event.
https://www.pabstmansion.com/2022/03/americasbest/

> **Book-usable formulation:** *The blue ribbon on the can commemorates a prize that was never awarded, at a competition that had no first place, in a year the ribbon had already been on the bottle for eleven years, described by a slogan written sixty years later.* Every clause of that sentence is sourced above.

## 1.3 The collapse, and the hipster resurrection (2001–2013)

**The collapse.** 18 million barrels (1977) → below 1 million (2001): roughly a **90–95% decline in 24 years**. The 1980s were, in trade-press phrasing, "a 20-year nosedive."
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pabst_Blue_Ribbon

**The turnaround, and the person.** Pabst hired **Neal Stewart** as senior brand manager around 2000–2001. In 2002 Pabst's numbers spiked anomalously in **Portland, Oregon**, unprompted by any marketing. Stewart went to Portland to find out why. What he found: "alternative people" in the Pacific Northwest had adopted the beer on their own. The brand had been chosen, not sold.
- https://www.fastcompany.com/3019247/05neal-stewart (403s to automated fetch; cited via search index)
- https://marketingmag.ca/brands/how-pabst-blue-ribbon-earned-its-hipster-cred-130748/
- https://vinepair.com/taplines-podcast/how-hipsters-found-pbr/
- https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna4562907

**The strategy — deliberate non-advertising.** Documented elements:
- **No TV, no NFL telecasts, no FM radio.** While competitors bought stadium tours, Pabst bought nothing at that scale.
- Stewart remade himself as "the Pabst guy" — street clothes, low key, pockets full of branded freebies, walking into Portland bars.
- **Bike messenger races and scooter rallies** sponsored directly. The documented method: find kids running a messenger race, hang out, offer them a banner for the next one.
- **Art gallery openings** sponsored — beer and hats delivered to openings, skate parties, juggling contests. The network was built of promoters *and* of "regular" people, explicitly including **artists promoting their own show openings**.
- Ads were bought — but for **bands, in local free papers**, not for the beer.
- MarketingSherpa's framing: "slipping money in the manner of a sly uncle to these small events."
- https://marketingsherpa.com/article/how-to/how-pabst-blue-ribbon-reversed | https://shepherdexpress.com/food/dining-preview/pabst-calls-comeback/ | https://thechallengerproject.com/blog/2020/pabst-blue-ribbon-case-study-marketing-interview | https://brittonmdg.com/blog/the-unlikely-resurgence-of-pabst-blue-ribbon/

**Result:** 2002 delivered PBR's **first volume increase since 1978**.

**The stated reason drinkers gave, in Portland and Pittsburgh:** they liked it for being *unpretentious and low profile* — "spontaneous and honest and never fake."
https://marketingmag.ca/brands/how-pabst-blue-ribbon-earned-its-hipster-cred-130748/

**More primary detail — Steve Nilsen (a.k.a. "Stix"), Lifestyle Marketing Manager at Pabst Blue Ribbon 2009–2018**, interviewed by The Challenger Project (2020). Direct quotes:
- *"Pabst didn't have the budgets to advertise. There was no marketing being shoved down people's throats."*
- *"We never told consumers — ever — what Pabst was: we let them decide for themselves."*
- *"Brands would pay big money to be at Coachella or South by Southwest or X Games. We were at all those events, but we didn't pay a dime to be there."*
- *"What's cool in Kansas City is different to what's cool in San Francisco and that's something that big brands don't understand."*
https://thechallengerproject.com/blog/2020/pabst-blue-ribbon-case-study-marketing-interview

**Neal Stewart, quoted contemporaneously (MarketingSherpa, 15 November 2004):**
- *"It was a forgotten brand with no marketing support."*
- *"We went for driving word of mouth and buzz."*
- **The line that is the whole book in one sentence:** *"They literally said to us, 'I love your beer but the minute you advertise on TV, I'm done with you.'"*
- *"We find a band we think fits our brand and plays good music, and mutual respect builds."*
https://marketingsherpa.com/article/how-to/how-pabst-blue-ribbon-reversed

**The tactic set, itemised (MarketingSherpa, 2004):** scooter rallies, kickball and dodgeball tournaments, fashion shows, bike messenger races, **art exhibition openings**. Plus **weekly quarter-page ads in local alternative papers across roughly 25 markets** — each ad listing different local bands playing that weekend, never the beer. Example cited: *The Onion* in the Denver market.

**Verified growth figures:**
- **2003: +15%** sales increase, with similar projections for 2004 (MarketingSherpa, 2004).
- Lost **90% of volume between 1978 and 2001** (same source).

⚠️ **Numbers to handle carefully — now resolved.** SEO-farm articles claim PBR grew "to nearly 92 million **barrels** by 2012." The **barrels** version is impossible — it would exceed total US beer production. The Challenger Project interview gives the correct unit: **"skyrocketed from selling less than 1m gallons in 2001 to topping 92m gallons in 2012."** Converting at 31 US gallons per beer barrel: **92 million gallons ≈ 2.97 million barrels** for the PBR brand at its 2012 peak. **Use gallons, or use "just under 3 million barrels." Never "92 million barrels."** *(The "less than 1m gallons in 2001" figure in that interview is itself garbled — the well-attested 2001 trough is under 1 million **barrels**, i.e. ~31 million gallons. Flag: this interviewee is loose with units. The barrels figures from Wikipedia/BA are the reliable series.)*

**Sale prices, both now sourced:** **$250 million** to Metropoulos in 2010; **$700 million** to Blue Ribbon Intermediate Holdings in 2014. https://thechallengerproject.com/blog/2020/pabst-blue-ribbon-case-study-marketing-interview

## 1.4 Why artists specifically

Four mechanisms, each separately evidenced:

**1. Price.** PBR occupied the sub-premium tier. The core cultural artefact is the cheap case — Pabst has sold cases as large as **180 cans**. https://www.foodnetwork.com/fn-dish/news/pabst-blue-ribbon-180-can-case-beer

**2. Anti-brand posture as a brand.** The beer's selling point was that it wasn't sold to you. This is not a reading — it is the documented strategy (1.3). Drinking it announced you were immune to advertising, using a product whose advertising strategy was to be immune to advertising.

**3. Working-class signalling by non-working-class people.** The frame is Bourdieu-derived. Cultural commentators identify the adoption of the symbols of another class as motivated by aspiration to rebel against one's own class. The sharper finding in the literature: *"it is not so much the taste in itself (what is liked) that matters, but being part of a discourse that is perceived as authentic (how it is liked)."*
- https://thesocietypages.org/socimages/2010/12/20/bourdieu-the-%E2%80%9Chipster%E2%80%9D-and-the-authenticity-of-taste/
- https://www.researchgate.net/publication/279955975_It's_really_not_hip_to_be_a_hipster_Negotiating_trends_and_authenticity_in_the_cultural_field
- http://forbes5.pitt.edu/article/postmodern-authenticity-and-hipster-identity

`[No peer-reviewed study using PBR specifically as the case object was located. The class-signalling reading is well-supported theory applied to PBR, not an empirical finding about PBR. Say so in the book.]`

**4. Pabst literally paid artists — and still does.** The **PBR Art Can Contest**, launched **2014**, is the direct institutional link between this beer and working artists:
- Over **300 million 16 oz cans** have carried contest artwork.
- Over **$100,000** awarded to emerging artists, **credited by name on the can**.
- First winner (2014): **Josh Holland**.
- 2021 winner: **Ashley Dreyfus**, a Boise **mixed-media artist** — her can ran on **30+ million cans**. *(The single most on-the-nose fact in this dossier for a book about a mixed-media artist.)*
- 2022: **Hannah Bunzey** (Asheville) beat ~**7,000 entrants from 120+ countries**.
- 10th edition: **$100,000 split across 10 winners** — a tenfold increase.
- https://brewpublic.com/beer-art-2/pabst-returns-with-the-pabst-blue-ribbon-art-can-contest/ | https://brewpublic.com/beer-art-2/seattle-artist-andrew-martinez-is-winner-of-pabst-blue-ribbon-10th-edition-art-can-contest/ | https://wlos.com/news/local/cheers-asheville-artist-named-one-of-pbrs-2022-art-can-contest-winners | https://www.slugmag.com/arts/art/interviews-features/beer-and-badass-babes-katie-mansfields-winning-pbr-can-design/ | https://www.packagingdigest.com/packaging-design/beer-artistry-6-years-of-winning-pabst-art-cans-gallery

## 1.6 PBR IN EUROPE AND POLAND — **the best material in this section**

**The finding is better than the premise of the question.** The question assumed PBR in Poland would be a rare, expensive import — an artist paying a premium for a cheap American beer. The record shows something stranger and funnier.

- **Pabst Blue Ribbon sold in Poland was produced by Van Pur S.A. under licence from Pabst Brewing Company, brewed at the brewery in Rakszawa**, Podkarpackie voivodeship.

Five breweries: **Jędrzejów, Koszalin, Łomża, Rakszawa, Zabrze** (some listings also give Braniewo). https://vanpur.com.pl/en/index.html | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Pur

### The China inversion — the same trick run the other way

Pabst sells **"Blue Ribbon 1844"** in China: a **720 ml brown bottle**, enamel label, all-malt reddish-brown strong ale, dry-hopped with Cascade, aged in **new uncharred American whiskey barrels**, retailing around **$44 a bottle**, served with a champagne flute. Pabst maintains it is a genuinely different, China-only brew. 1844 = the founding year.
https://business.time.com/2010/07/28/sell-more-by-rebranding-in-china/ | https://www.gawkerarchives.com/5592399/pabst-blue-ribbon-will-run-you-44-a-bottle-in-china | https://www.beeradvocate.com/beer/profile/16236/62853/ (listed under **Guangdong Blue Ribbon Group**) | https://worksdesigngroup.com/blog/pbr-brand-repositioning/

**So the same trademark is simultaneously:** a $44 luxury ale in China, an €8.45/litre import affectation in Germany, a 4 zł Polish supermarket lager brewed in Rakszawa, and a working-class signifier in Portland. The label means whatever the local class system needs it to mean. That is the thesis.

## 1.7 The boilermaker, and the product-line flailing

### "PBR and a shot"

- **Etymology.** "Boilermaker" as an occupation — a maker and repairer of engine boilers — enters English in **1814** (some sources give the 1830s for the steam-engine sense, per the OED). https://vinepair.com/wine-blog/the-history-of-the-boilermaker-otherwise-known-as-a-shot-and-a-beer/
- **As a drink.** Some sources date the shot-plus-beer application to **1910**. The longer form **"boilermaker's delight"** designated the strong, cheap combination, joked to be strong enough to strip the scale from the inside of a boiler. In the **1930s** it was called **"a Boilermaker and His Helper."** By the **1940s** it was standard on bar menus nationwide. https://www.thetakeout.com/a-brief-history-of-the-boilermaker-beer-and-shot-combo-1849071213/ | https://barwarriors.com/history-of-the-boilermaker/ | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boilermaker_(beer_cocktail) | https://www.americanprohibitionmuseum.com/cocktails/boilermaker
- **The class reading is explicit in the sources:** "the pairing of a strong shot of hard alcohol with a cold beer has always been associated with the working class." The canonical dive-bar version is **cheap whiskey or bourbon with a PBR or a Bud**; the gentrified version swaps in Sazerac or Four Roses and a craft beer. **The 2002 spirits excise cut is the hinge of the whole story.** Excise on ethyl alcohol was cut **30% in 2002**; spirits consumption went 1.7 L (2002) → 2.4 L (2003) → 3.4 L (2008) — **a doubling in six years.**

**Affordability, the killer statistic:** a Polish minimum wage bought **32** half-litre bottles of 40% vodka in **2001**, and **107** in **2021** — a **tripling of real affordability in twenty years.** Both from https://www.termedia.pl/Alcohol-as-a-public-health-threat-in-Poland-rising-consumption-weakening-policy-and-increasing-mortality,100,57221,1,1.html

## 2.3 MAŁPKI — and the answer to "was anything actually enacted?"

### ✅ WHAT IS ACTUALLY IN FORCE (as of 17 August 2026)
**The "opłata małpkowa" has existed since 1 January 2021.** It is **25 PLN per litre of 100% alcohol** in packaging up to **300 ml** — about **1 PLN on a 100 ml bottle, 2 PLN on a 200 ml bottle**. **That rate has not changed.** https://www.money.pl/gospodarka/rzad-wprowadzi-oplate-malpkowa-podano-mozliwy-termin-7057552772508288a.html

### ❌ WHAT HAS *NOT* HAPPENED
- **No ban on 100 ml or small-format bottles has been enacted.** A PiS amendment to ban packaging under 300 ml was tabled in the Sejm Health Committee in **July 2026** and **deferred to the second reading**, because such a ban triggers an **EU notification requirement** that would delay the whole bill. https://www.bankier.pl/wiadomosc/Koniec-reklam-piwa-Komisja-Zdrowia-przyjela-projekt-zmian-9175190.html
- **No increase in the levy has been enacted.** Deputy Finance Minister **Jarosław Neneman** announced a proposed **fourfold increase (25 → ~100 PLN/litre pure alcohol)** at a Konfederacja Lewiatan Tax Council congress on **16 June 2026**. Draft is in the government legislative register, slated for Cabinet adoption **Q3/Q4 2026**, effective **from 2027 at the earliest**. https://www.dlahandlu.pl/raporty-i-analizy/wyzsza-akcyza-i-drozsze-malpki-jest-nowy-plan-rzadu,171762.html
- A Ministry of Health idea to restrict ≤300 ml packaging to bottles/cans only is `[UNVERIFIED as to legislative form]`.

> **BOTTOM LINE: nothing about małpki has been enacted since 2021. Any claim of a ban, or of a higher rate, as of August 2026 is FALSE.**

## 2.5 ALCOHOL POLICY

### PARPA → KCPU: ✅ VERIFIED, and it matters for citation hygiene
**PARPA (Państwowa Agencja Rozwiązywania Problemów Alkoholowych) was merged with KBPN (Krajowe Biuro ds. Przeciwdziałania Narkomanii) into KCPU (Krajowe Centrum Przeciwdziałania Uzależnieniom), which began operating 1 January 2022.** Legal basis: **ustawa z 17 listopada 2021 r.** amending the public-health act. https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krajowe_Centrum_Przeciwdzia%C5%82ania_Uzale%C5%BCnieniom · https://www.parpa.pl/index.php/aktualnosci/1436-polaczenie-parpa-i-kbpn-w-krajowe-centrum-przeciwdzialania-uzaleznieniom
**parpa.pl still exists as an archive and still carries the statistics page. Post-2021 data must be cited to KCPU, not PARPA.**

### Advertising — what is allowed *now*
Under **Art. 13¹ ustawy o wychowaniu w trzeźwości i przeciwdziałaniu alkoholizmowi**, advertising and promotion of alcoholic drinks is **banned — except beer**. Beer advertising on **TV, radio, cinema and theatre is permitted only between 20:00 and 06:00**. It must not target or depict minors, must not link drinking to physical fitness or driving, and must not claim medicinal, stimulant or calming properties. https://lexlege.pl/ustawa-o-wychowaniu-w-trzezwosci-i-przeciwdzialaniu-alkoholizmowi/art-13-1/
The **1982** advertising ban was **loosened in 2001–2003**, which is when broadcast beer advertising became possible.

### The 2025/2026 tightening — status: **NOT YET LAW**
- **September 2025:** two MPs' bills filed in the Sejm — one from **Lewica**, one from **Polska 2050**.
- **January 2026:** first reading; both referred to the **Health Committee**, then a subcommittee.
- **July 2026:** subcommittee adopted the **Lewica bill as the lead text**; the **Sejm Health Committee approved the merged bill** and sent it to second reading.
- **Content:** a **total ban on advertising and promotion of all alcoholic drinks, including beer and 0.0% beer**; "promotion" redefined to include **discounts, rebates and bonuses**, killing "12 beers + 12 free" shop offers; **fines up to 1 million PLN**; higher licence fees; night-time restrictions on petrol-station sales.
- A **Ministry of Health** variant proposed cutting the beer advertising window by three hours, to **23:00–06:00**.
- **Rejected:** a proposed **24-hour ban on alcohol sales at petrol stations** was voted down by coalition, PiS and the presidential camp alike.
- **Status as of August 2026: awaiting second reading. NOT LAW.**

### Night-time sales bans — precise dates
- **Legal basis:** gminas may set night-time retail bans. **176 gminas adopted them between 2018 and 2024 (7.1% of Polish local governments)** — most in Mazowsze (26), Małopolska (20), Wielkopolska (20). ### Excise 2022–2026 — the indexation schedule, and the veto
- The **"mapa akcyzowa"** in force since January 2022 set **+10% in 2022**, then **+5% per year 2023–2027** on ethyl alcohol, beer, wine, fermented drinks and intermediate products. https://ksiegowosc.infor.pl/wiadomosci/5338608,Podwyzki-akcyzy-na-alkohol-i-tyton.html
- **Beer excise, PLN per hectolitre:** 2022 — **385.00**; 2023 — **404.00**; 2024 — **424.00**; 2025 — **445.00**; 2026 — **467.00**.
- **December 2025:** the Sejm passed an amendment raising 2026 to **+15%** and 2027 to **+10%**. **President Karol Nawrocki vetoed it on 18 December 2025**, saying: *"I cannot agree to an increase in excise tax introduced without time for entrepreneurs and consumers to prepare. This is chaos and proof of poor government financial management."* The Finance Ministry had projected **~1.96 bn PLN** in extra revenue; consumer impact was estimated at **+16 groszy per bottle of beer** and **+3 PLN per bottle of vodka**. The veto also killed a rise in the sugar levy (*opłata cukrowa*). https://tvn24.pl/biznes/z-kraju/nawrocki-wetuje-podwyzke-akcyzy-na-alkohol-st8808784 · https://www.rynekzdrowia.pl/Prawo/Kolejne-weto-prezydenta-Nawrocki-to-sieganie-po-pieniadze-Polakow,279310,2.html
- ✅ **CONSEQUENCE: the default +5% took effect on 1 January 2026, NOT +15%.** Confirmed: https://www.bankier.pl/wiadomosc/Wzrosla-akcyza-na-wyroby-alkoholowe-i-papierosy-9062927.html (1 Jan 2026). For contrast, the same date brought **+20% on cigarettes, +30% on rolling tobacco, +50% on e-liquids** — **alcohol was spared and tobacco was not.**

## 2.7 SECTION 2 SOURCE HYGIENE — the eight things not to get wrong

1. **Never mix the 8.8 and the 11.5.** GUS/KCPU = whole population, registered sales. WHO = 15+, includes unrecorded. Always name the basis and the year.
2. **The 1990 GUS figure does not exist** — the domestic series starts 1992. Use WHO's 8.16 for 1990.
3. **"Highest consumption in history" is wrong for the 10 L claim** — 1981 (11.21) was higher.
4. **The 2008 "peak" is a myth on the WHO series** — 2019 (10.99) and 2021 (10.96) were higher.
5. **Nothing about małpki has been enacted since 2021.** The levy is unchanged at 25 PLN/L.
6. **Beer market shares differ by ten points depending on volume vs shelf space.** Say which.
7. **Craft brewery counts are genuinely unreliable in Poland after 2021** — the reporting infrastructure does not exist.
8. **Alcohol excise did not rise 15% in 2026. It rose 5%,** because of the 18 December 2025 presidential veto.

## 3.3 How muralists actually work — the practical mechanics

**Access equipment.** Three classes: truck-mounted **boom lifts (*podnośniki koszowe*)**, **scissor lifts** for flat wide surfaces, and **telescopic/articulated booms** for reaching around obstacles. The decisive practical fact for the book: **the operator must hold UDT certification** (Urząd Dozoru Technicznego); rental firms usually supply a licensed operator either in the price or as an extra. **Scaffolding is rejected for tall buildings because erection time makes it uneconomic** — lifts let a wall be done "in a few days instead of a few weeks." https://opoland.pl/blog/wynajem-podnosnikow-do-malowania-murali-praktyczny-przewodnik-dla-artystow-i-firm

**Materials.** Exterior murals use *farby elewacyjne* (facade paints) — acrylic, silicate or silicone — plus artist aerosols, or mixed technique. https://wypiszwymaluj.com/jak-wyglada-realizacja-muralu-etapy-pracy-od-projektu-do-gotowej-sciany/
- **Śnieżka Elewacyjna** (acrylic facade): coverage **up to 9 m²/l per coat**, **two coats recommended**, brush/roller/airless, recoat after **~4 h**, application at **+10 °C to +30 °C** substrate and air, no fog/rain/snow; sold in 2.5 / 5 / 10 L. **Cement render must cure 4 weeks, gypsum 2 weeks, before painting.** https://www.sniezka.pl/produkt/sniezka-elewacyjna/
- **Caparol Muresko** (silicone facade): minimum application and drying temperature **+5 °C**; at 20 °C / 65 % RH surface-dry and recoatable in **4–6 h**, fully cured after **~3 days**. https://www.caparol.pl/innowacje/rozwiazania-systemowe/wskazowki-dotyczace-malowania-elewacji-farbami-muresko-premium-i-muresko-coolprotect
- **The +5 °C vs +10 °C split is the real seasonal constraint.** With a silicone paint you can push into early spring and late autumn; with a standard acrylic you cannot. Painting is also ruled out in rain, strong wind, frost, very high humidity, and harsh direct sun.

**Paint quantity — usable numbers.** Rule-of-thumb facade consumption: **100 m² of smooth, primed wall = 17–25 litres for two coats**; on textured *"baranek"* render **22–33 litres**. Premium paints at ~12 m²/l bring 100 m² down to ~16.7 l; priming to even out absorbency cuts consumption **15–20 %**. Extrapolating: **a 1,000 m² gable needs roughly 170–330 litres of base/facade paint for two coats**, before any spray work on top. https://krismag.pl/100m2-elewacji-ile-farby-kupic-realne-zuzycie-17-35l | https://adrem.org.pl/ile-potrzeba-farby-na-100m2-elewacji/

**Spray brands.** ⚠️ **Correction:** **Loop Colors is Italian, not Polish** — made by **Ital G.E.T.E.**, an Italian aerosol manufacturer with 40+ years' history, ISO 9001/14001/45001 certified. https://loopcolors.com/ — `[Whether Montana and Molotow are the actual working brands of Polish muralists: UNVERIFIED. Both are standard graffiti-market brands, but this needs an interview, not a search.]`

**Process and duration.** Twelve stages: brief → site survey → design → visualisation → quote → permissions → surface preparation → equipment logistics → transferring the design (grid or projection) → painting → inspection → protective coat. Wall prep can include pressure washing, stripping old paint, crack filling and priming. Painting durations:
- **under 20 m² = 1–3 days**
- **30–50 m² = 2–5 days**
- **50–100 m² = 4–10 days**
- **very large = 1–3 weeks plus planning**
https://wypiszwymaluj.com/jak-wyglada-realizacja-muralu-etapy-pracy-od-projektu-do-gotowej-sciany/
**A 10-storey gable sits at the top of that scale.** Corroborating real jobs: the 24-storey Łódź Witcher took **eight painters over two weeks**; **Inti's 11-storey Łódź wall took over 11 days solo.**

**Anti-graffiti coatings and lifespan.**
- **Sacrificial ("tracone") coatings:** up to **5 years** in tunnels and underpasses; outdoors, exposed to sun and rain, **about one year**. One product (Hydrograff HP) is rated for **8–12 cleanings** with acetone/graffiti remover.
- **Permanent coatings:** typically **8–10 years**; a two-component polyurethane system holds **5 years or up to 50 cleanings**; some hydrophobic coatings claim **up to 10 years**.
- **The caveat that matters for murals:** the underlying paint must be well-bonded and wash-resistant, or steam-cleaning graffiti off strips the artwork with it.
https://umyjemto.pl/ | https://www.liderbudowlany.pl/artykul/materialy-budowlane/farby-kleje-lakiery-emalie/skuteczne-zabezpieczenia-antygraffiti/ | https://trokentech.pl/mury/powloki-antygraffiti/

Sources say only "depends on substrate preparation and protection."]` **In practice the binding constraint is not paint failure but demolition, thermomodernisation and repainting** — see Zaspa, and "Odzyski" in Łódź, gone within a decade.

### **Is it a living?** — the honest answer

# 4. THE SCIENCE OF ALCOHOL AND CREATIVITY

> ⚠️ **READ THIS FIRST. The headline finding did not survive replication.** The 2012 "Uncorking the muse" result — the study every article about drunk creativity cites — was tested in 2020 by a preregistered, double-blind, placebo-controlled study with **N = 125**, powered for small effects. It found **no effect on the Remote Associates Test whatsoever (p = .98)**. This is the single most important fact in this section, and a book that cites Jarosz 2012 without citing Benedek & Zöhrer 2020 is not being honest.

## 4.1 Jarosz, Colflesh & Wiley (2012) — "Uncorking the muse"

**Full citation:** Jarosz, A. F., Colflesh, G. J. H., & Wiley, J. (2012). Uncorking the muse: Alcohol intoxication facilitates creative problem solving. *Consciousness and Cognition*, 21(1), 487–493.
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.concog.2012.01.002 · **PMID:** 22285424
Classified by the journal as a **"Short Communication."** Received 17 Aug 2011; online 30 Jan 2012. Dept. of Psychology, University of Illinois at Chicago.
Full text: https://www.sfu.ca/~kathleea/docs/Uncorking%20the%20muse-%20Alcohol%20intoxication%20facilitates%20creative%20problem%20solving%20-%20Jarosz,%20Colflesh,%20Wiley.pdf · https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1053810012000037

### Sample — CONFIRMED, and small
- **40 male social drinkers, aged 21–30.** 20 intoxicated, 20 sober. **All-male, exactly as suspected.**
- Recruited **via Craigslist and the university community.**
- Three extra participants were run in the sober condition to complete matching; **only the 40 matched participants were analysed.**
- Excluded if they scored above 3 on the **Short Michigan Alcoholism Screening Test** or had medical contraindications. Eligibility required typical consumption of ≥2 drinks on any day of the week over the prior 3 months.
- Groups **matched on working memory capacity (Operation Span)** measured sober: sober M = .63, SD = .16; intoxicated M = .63, SD = .18; t(38) = −.04, ns.

### The dose — CONFIRMED
- **100-proof Smirnoff vodka**, calibrated by weight at **.88 g/kg body weight**, mixed **in front of the participant at 1:3 vodka-to-cranberry-juice**, in **three equal doses over 10-minute periods**.
- Participants first ate a weight-adjusted **snack of bagels** and watched the animated film ***Ratatouille*** while drinking.
- Post-drinking breathalyzer M = .069 (printed "SD = .14" — ⚠️ **almost certainly a typo for .014**, since the stated range was .044–.094).
- **BAC immediately before the RAT: M = .075, SD = .008, range .059–.091.** The 0.075 figure is confirmed; it is the **peak** measurement, taken right before the critical task, about an hour in.

### The task — CONFIRMED
- **Remote Associates Test (RAT), 15 items**, computer-presented, **1-minute limit per problem.**
- Items from Bowden & Jung-Beeman (2003b) and Wiley (1998). Prior solution rates **.17 to .77**: 5 easy (≥60% solved), 5 intermediate, 5 difficult (≤30%).
- **Feeling-of-insight ratings on a 1–7 scale.** 1 = worked it out strategically; 7 = *"the solution came to you suddenly… 'It popped into my head'; 'Of course!'; 'That's so obvious.'"*

### Results — EXACT NUMBERS
| Measure | Intoxicated | Sober | Statistic |
|---|---|---|---|
| RAT proportion correct | **M = .58, SD = .13** | **M = .42, SD = .16** | t(38) = 3.43, **p = .001, d = 1.08** |
| Solution time | **M = 11.54 s, SD = 3.75** | **M = 15.24 s, SD = 5.57** | t(38) = 2.47, **p = .02, d = .78** |
| Mean insight rating | M = 3.98 | M = 3.35 | t(38) = 1.78, **p < .08 — MARGINAL, NOT SIGNIFICANT** |
| Count of solutions rated insightful (5–7) | higher | lower | t(38) = 2.14, **p = .039, d = .69** |
| Count rated analytic (1–3) | — | — | t(38) = 1.18, **ns** |

⚠️ **Important nuance on the "sudden insight" claim.** The answer is a *qualified* yes. **The overall mean insight-rating difference was only marginal (p < .08).** The significant result was the narrower analysis of *how many* correctly solved problems fell in the 5–7 "insightful" band (p = .039). **Popular coverage routinely reports this as a clean finding; the paper's own primary insight test did not reach conventional significance.**

### The control — and the fatal gap
There was **no separate creative control task.** Two things served that role:
1. A **sober comparison group** doing every identical task including *Ratatouille*, but eating no snack and drinking nothing. **CRITICALLY, THERE WAS NO PLACEBO CONDITION.** The authors state: *"As participants do not find a BAC of .07 to be credible in placebo studies, a placebo design was not employed (Martin & Sayette, 1993)."*
2. The **Operation Span (OSpan)** working memory task, run twice (before and after drinking) as the mechanism measure. No main effect of condition (F < 1); main effect of timing F(1,34) = 4.14, p = .05, η² = .11; **significant interaction F(1,34) = 5.23, p = .03, η² = .14** — **sober participants showed the normal practice improvement on the second span task; intoxicated participants did not.**

### Limitations — several are severe
- **All-male sample, n = 40, 20 per cell.** Authors note male samples are common "due to IRB complications of dealing with unknown pregnancies in females and differential dosage levels."
- **Single dose, single BAC (0.075).** No dose-response curve.
- **No placebo, no blinding.** Expectancy is entirely unaddressed — fatal given Lapp et al. 1994 (§4.2).
- **No control for the drinking ritual itself.**
- **d = 1.08 from n = 20 per group is the classic profile of an effect that shrinks on replication** — and it did.
- Funded by a **Psi Chi Graduate Research Grant** to the first author; authors state the funder played no role.

## 4.3 The insight vs. analysis distinction, and defocused attention

### Wiley & Jarosz's own theoretical statement
- **Wiley, J., & Jarosz, A. F. (2012). Working Memory Capacity, Attentional Focus, and Problem Solving.** *Current Directions in Psychological Science*, 21(4), 258–262. DOI 10.1177/0963721412447622 · https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0963721412447622
- **Wiley, J., & Jarosz, A. F. (2012). How working memory capacity affects problem solving.** In B. H. Ross (Ed.), *Psychology of Learning and Motivation*, Vol. 56, 185–227. DOI 10.1016/B978-0-12-394393-4.00006-6

Mechanism, verbatim: *"Increased attentional control implies that one is better able to screen out peripheral information, which, while useful during analytical problem solving, would be disadvantageous in a situation where the assimilation of information outside of the perceived problem space may be useful."* And from the 2012 discussion: *"with intoxication, individuals may opt for more passive or associative approaches to solving the RAT problems, which in turn ends up to be beneficial."*

Jarosz et al. also invoke a **hemispheric account** (Beeman & Bowden 2000; Bowden & Jung-Beeman 2003a): the **left hemisphere uses fine semantic coding** to activate a few close associates; the **right hemisphere uses coarse coding** to activate a broad, diffuse array of remote associates. Reduced attentional control lets weak right-hemisphere remote associates surface. ⚠️ **This is theoretical interpretation in the paper, not something the study measured.**

### Supporting evidence for reduced-control benefits — verified
- **Reverberi, Toraldo, D'Agostini & Skrap (2005).** *Brain*, 128, 2882–2890. **Lateral-frontal-lobe patients** performed equally to healthy controls on easy matchstick arithmetic but showed *"a considerable advantage on difficult items, solving almost 40% more problems."* **Literal brain damage to the control system improving insight performance.**
- **DeCaro, Van Stockum & Wieth (2016). When higher working memory capacity hinders insight.** *JEP: LMC*, 42(1), 39–49. PMID 26120772. Higher WMC associated with **less accurate** insight problem solving on matchstick arithmetic.
- **Ricks, Turley-Ames & Wiley (2007).** *Memory & Cognition*, 35, 1456–1462. High-WMC individuals more likely to be **fixated** by misleading associates primed by prior knowledge.
- **Kim, Hasher & Zacks (2007).** *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review*, 14, 301–305. **Older adults**, with inhibitory deficits, were *better* than college students at exploiting hints embedded in distracting information from a previous, ostensibly unrelated task.
- **Beilock & DeCaro (2007); DeCaro, Thomas & Beilock (2008):** low-WMC individuals faster to abandon effortful strategies for simple heuristics on complex category-induction tasks.

### Wieth & Zacks (2011) — the time-of-day study — VERIFIED IN FULL
**Citation:** Wieth, M. B., & Zacks, R. T. (2011). Time of day effects on problem solving: When the non-optimal is optimal. *Thinking & Reasoning*, 17(4), 387–401. **DOI:** 10.1080/13546783.2011.625663. Albion College and Michigan State University. Funded by **NIA Grant R37 AG04306**.
Full text: https://creativiteach.me/wp-content/uploads/2013/12/weithzacks.pdf

- **N = 428** students (mean age 20.41, SD 1.91). Chronotype via the **Horne & Östberg (1976) Morningness–Eveningness Questionnaire**.
- ⚠️ **Only 223 with a clear circadian preference were analysed — and here is the study's biggest weakness: 195 evening types vs only 28 morning types.** 205 neutral types were excluded.
- Sessions: morning (8:30–9:30 am) or late afternoon (4:00–5:30 pm). **Three insight problems** (Fake Coin, Prisoner, Water Lilies) and **three analytic problems** (Age, Bachelor, Flower), 4 minutes each, from Metcalfe & Wiebe (1987) and Wieth & Burns (2006). Inter-rater reliability κ = .91.

**Results (exact):**
- Problem type main effect: F(1,221) = 8.65, p = .004, η² = .04 (analytic easier overall).
- **Time of day main effect: F(1,221) = 1.06, p = .304 — none.**
- **Interaction: F(1,221) = 6.51, p = .011, η² = .03.**
- **Insight: non-optimal M = .42 vs optimal M = .33; t(221) = 2.37, p = .019, η² = .03.**
- **Analytic: non-optimal M = .43 vs optimal M = .47; t(221) = .95, p = .342, ns.**
- A small pilot (n = 34): non-optimal M = .51 vs optimal M = .26; t(32) = 2.68, p = .01, η² = .18.

✅ **Confirmed** — but note the effect is small (η² = .03, a 9-percentage-point difference) and the morning-type cell is only 28 people.

### Salvi, Bricolo, Kounios, Bowden & Beeman (2016) — "Insight solutions are correct more often than analytic solutions"
*Thinking & Reasoning*, 22(4), 443–460. **DOI:** 10.1080/13546783.2016.1141798 · **PMID:** 27667960. Online 5 Feb 2016.
Full text: https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.northwestern.edu/dist/a/699/files/2015/11/Salvi_etal_Insight-is-right_TR2016-2n3ns9l.pdf

| Exp. | Task | N | Insight correct | Analytic correct | Statistic |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 120 Remote Associates, 15 s | 38 (22 F, M age 20.12) | **93.7%** | **78.3%** | t(37) = 5.66, d = .99, p < .001 |
| 2 | 180 anagrams, 16 s, EEG | 51 (25 F, M age 20.5) | **97.6%** | **91.9%** | t(50) = 2.78, d = 1.50, p < .01 |
| 3 | 88 Italian rebus puzzles, 15 s | 110 (81 F, M age 21.2) | **78.5%** | **63.2%** | t(109) = 6.08, d = .66, p < .001 |
| 4 | **50 fragmented line drawings (VISUAL)**, 15 s | 27 (21 F, M age 22.3) | **78.4%** | **41.5%** | t(26) = 7.47, **d = 1.85**, p < .001 |

Mechanism: **insight is all-or-none, analysis is incremental.** Analytic solutions therefore contain more **errors of commission** (deadline guesses); insight solutions contain more **timeouts** (errors of omission). In Exp. 1, accuracy dropped from 10.5% errors in the 2–10 s window to **34.1% errors in the final 5 s**, and those late errors were overwhelmingly labelled analytic (29.4% vs 4.7%).

⚠️ **Crucial for the book's argument:** the aha-accuracy finding is about **self-reported phenomenology**, and it does **NOT** license the inference *"alcohol → more aha feelings → therefore better ideas."* **Nobody has tested that chain.**

### Counter-evidence on the WMC–insight story
- **Chuderski, A., & Jastrzębski, J. (2018). Much ado about aha!: Insight problem solving is strongly related to working memory capacity and reasoning ability.** *JEP: General*, 147(2), 257–281. PMID 29058940. **WMC predicted 51.8% of variance in insight problem solving** — a strong *positive* relationship, **opposite to the defocused-attention prediction.**
- **Gong, Z. et al. (2023)**, meta-analysis, *Journal of Intelligence*, 11(1), PMID 36662145: significantly **positive** WMC–creativity association (r = .083).
- Benedek & Zöhrer (2020) summarise the field honestly: individual-differences research has *"accumulated robust evidence for a positive relationship between intelligence and creative thinking performance,"* and correlations with executive functions *"depend strongly on the type task."*

> **The honest framing: the defocused-attention account is a LIVE HYPOTHESIS WITH MIXED SUPPORT, not a settled finding. The single best-powered, preregistered, placebo-controlled test of its alcohol prediction came out null.**

## 4.4 Counter-evidence: working memory, sleep, long-term output

### Working memory
- **Jarosz's own 2012 data:** intoxicated participants **lost the normal practice gain** on the second OSpan (interaction F(1,34) = 5.23, p = .03, η² = .14).
- **Colflesh, Jarosz & Wiley (2010):** intoxication to 0.07 BAC linked to **decreases in WMC** on complex span tasks.
- ⚠️ **But Benedek & Zöhrer (2020) found NO working-memory impairment at BAC 0.03 or 0.06** (F(2,122) = 0.35, p = .70) and explicitly flag the discrepancy with Jarosz (2012) at 0.07 and Benedek et al. (2017) at 0.03.
- They also note *"consistent impairments of cognitive functioning [are found] only at high BAC levels (Dry et al., 2012; Peterson et al., 1990)."*

### Sleep architecture — VERIFIED, and this is the strongest counter-evidence in the section
**Ebrahim, I. O., Shapiro, C. M., Williams, A. J., & Fenwick, P. B. (2013). Alcohol and sleep I: effects on normal sleep.** *Alcoholism: Clinical and Experimental Research*, 37(4), 539–549. **PMID:** 23347102 · **DOI:** 10.1111/acer.12006 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23347102/

Abstract verbatim:
> *"At all dosages, alcohol causes a reduction in sleep onset latency, a more consolidated first half sleep and an increase in sleep disruption in the second half of sleep."*
> *"Total night REM sleep percentage is decreased in the majority of studies at moderate and high doses with no clear trend apparent at low doses. **The onset of the first REM sleep period is significantly delayed at all doses** and appears to be the most recognizable effect of alcohol on REM sleep followed by the reduction in total night REM sleep."*
> *"The majority of studies, across dose, age and gender, confirm an **increase in slow wave sleep (SWS) in the first half of the night** relative to baseline values… Total night SWS is increased at high alcohol doses across gender and age groups."*

⚠️ **Correction to the common framing:** slow-wave sleep in the first half of the night is **increased**, not merely "changed," and REM is **delayed at all doses** and **reduced at moderate-to-high doses.**

This matters enormously, because Jarosz et al. themselves cite the sleep-and-insight literature approvingly:
- **Wagner, Gais, Haider, Verleger & Born (2004). Sleep inspires insight.** *Nature*, 427, 352–355. DOI 10.1038/nature02223. Participants who slept were **more than twice as likely** to discover a hidden shortcut in the number-reduction task.
- **Cai, Mednick, Harrison, Kanady & Mednick (2009). REM, not incubation, improves creativity by priming associative networks.** *PNAS*, 106, 10130–10134. DOI 10.1073/pnas.0900271106. **REM sleep specifically** improved RAT performance via primed associative networks; wake and non-REM sleep did not.

> **THE SELF-UNDERMINING ARGUMENT — the best single idea in this section for the book.** Jarosz's own proposed mechanism (broadened associative spread, right-hemisphere remote associates) is *exactly* the mechanism the REM literature attributes to REM sleep. **And alcohol is the most reliable known suppressor and delayer of REM.** A drink that buys you a 16-percentage-point RAT gain for one hour costs you the REM period that produces the same effect overnight, for free.
> ⚠️ **NO STUDY HAS DIRECTLY TESTED THIS TRADE-OFF. This framing is an inference, not a published finding. Mark it as such in the book.**

## 4.5 "Write drunk, edit sober" — **THE ATTRIBUTION IS FALSE**

✅ **The hypothesis in the brief is correct and confirmed.** Primary source: Quote Investigator, "Quote Origin: Write Drunk, Revise Sober," 21 September 2016 — https://quoteinvestigator.com/2016/09/21/write-drunk/

### The actual origin
**Peter De Vries, *Reuben, Reuben* (Little, Brown, 1964), Chapter 21, page 242.** Spoken by **Gowan McGland**, a dissolute Scottish/Welsh poet character partially modelled on **Dylan Thomas**. The actual wording:

> **"Sometimes I write drunk and revise sober," he had said, "and sometimes I write sober and revise drunk. But you have to have both elements in creation — the Apollonian and the Dionysian, or spontaneity and restraint, emotion and discipline."**

**Three things the popular version destroys:**
1. It says **"revise,"** not "edit."
2. **The original is SYMMETRICAL** — De Vries's character does it in *both* directions. The slogan version amputates half the sentence, which is the half that makes it a thought rather than a licence.
3. It is a **description of a fictional drunk's practice, not advice**, and it is **framed ironically** — McGland is depicted removing lines he suspected he had written while drunk. https://www.grunge.com/1385802/did-ernest-hemingway-really-write-drunk-edit-sober/

### The chain of transmission
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **1964** | De Vries, *Reuben, Reuben* — correctly attributed to the character |
| **1966** | *The Writer* magazine credits Gowan McGland |
| **1984** | *Sacramento Bee* reports "Write drunk and edit sober" as unattributed newsroom wisdom |
| **2005** | **Earliest known attribution to Hemingway — in a Usenet post to rec.woodworking**, with no evidence offered |
| **2013** | *The Guardian* refers to it as "Hemingway's much-quoted advice," cementing the error |

QI's verdict: *"Researchers have been unable to find this saying in the output of Ernest Hemingway who died in 1961, and it is unlikely that he ever said it or wrote it."*

> **The joke writes itself: the most famous piece of advice about drinking and creativity was invented by a novelist, put in the mouth of a fictional alcoholic as a warning, cut in half, and attributed to a dead man on a woodworking newsgroup in 2005.**

### What Hemingway actually said
**The documented denial** — interview by **Edward Stafford**, conducted before Hemingway's death in 1961, published in ***Writer's Digest* in 1964**, reported by Writer's Digest itself (https://www.writersdigest.com/be-inspired/did-hemingway-say-write-drunk-edit-sober-nope-he-preferred-to-write-sober):

> **"Have you ever heard of anyone who drank while he worked? You're thinking of Faulkner. He does sometimes — and I can tell right in the middle of a page when he's had his first one."**

Additional attributed statements ⚠️ **`[LOWER CONFIDENCE — sourced from Grunge, a popular site; verify against primary texts before printing]`**:
- *A Moveable Feast* (1964, posthumous): **"My training was never to drink after dinner nor before I wrote nor while I was writing."**
- Letter to Charles Scribner, on counting his day's words: **"I always count them when I knock off and am drinking the first whiskey and soda"** — the drink came *after* the work.
- Letter to his mother (1927): **"I have never been a drunk nor even a steady drinker… all I want is tranquility and a chance to write."**

**Corroborating detail from a peer-reviewed source — Ludwig (1990), p. 955:** *"Ernest Hemingway was able to write his 500 words every morning despite the aftermath of any heavy drinking the night before."* Ludwig classifies Hemingway under **"alcohol intake indirectly increases creative output"** and notes elsewhere that *"Ernest Hemingway often drank to relieve depression or pain and wrote when his level of distress was low."*

> **So: Hemingway drank heavily, but the documented pattern is write-in-the-morning, drink-in-the-afternoon — the exact opposite of the slogan bearing his name.**

## 4.6 Alcohol and VISUAL artists — **there is essentially no research. Say so plainly.**

A systematic Europe PMC query crossing alcohol/creativity with visual art, painters and artists returned **zero experimental studies of alcohol's effect on visual art production.** Hits were mood-induction studies (Palmiero et al., 2023, *Brain Sciences*, PMID 36672130 — sadness and drawing, **no alcohol**), art-therapy reviews, performing-artist wellbeing reviews, and an opium history paper (Wolf, 2010, PMID 20375523).

**Everything in the experimental alcohol–creativity literature is verbal or verbal-adjacent:**
- Jarosz et al. (2012): Remote Associates Test — **verbal**.
- Benedek et al. (2017), Benedek & Zöhrer (2020): RAT + Alternate Uses Task — **both verbal**.
- Gustafson (1991), Norlander & Gustafson (1998), Lang et al. (1984): idea-generation/fluency — **verbal**.

**The two partial exceptions:**
1. **Lapp, Collins & Izzo (1994)**, PMID 8067507 — **the ONLY identified alcohol study using a visual/spatial creative task** (arranging wildflower pictures by colour, shape and number). **Its result: alcohol had NO pharmacological effect. Only *expectancy* increased novelty and structural recombination.** **The one visual-domain alcohol experiment found no drug effect at all.**
2. **Roe (1946)**, described in **Ludwig (1990), p. 954** ⚠️ `[SECONDARY SOURCE — Roe not accessed directly]`: **17 established artists** interviewed. All drank. **"All but one avoided drinking while painting. All but one of 17 artists regarded the short-term effects of alcohol as deleterious… and none used alcohol to overcome technical difficulties."** Ludwig adds: *"The general sentiment was that **alcohol provided the freedom for painting but impaired the discipline**."* He also reports a style correlation: **moderate drinkers were realistic painters; steady social drinkers had a wide range of styles; excessive drinkers showed greater shifts in style.** This is an ~80-year-old interview study, not an experiment — **but it is the closest thing that exists to evidence about painters, and its finding is that painters themselves say don't.**

Also relevant, though not about alcohol: **Salvi et al. (2016) Experiment 4** used a genuinely **visual** insight task (fragmented line drawings, Snodgrass & Vanderwart 1980) and found the insight-accuracy advantage **strongest of all four experiments** (78.4% vs 41.5%, d = 1.85). So the insight/analysis distinction generalises beyond words — **but nobody has ever put alcohol and a visual creativity task together except Lapp et al., who found nothing.**

> **BOTTOM LINE FOR §4:** *The entire empirical case for "alcohol helps creativity" rests on one verbal task — the Remote Associates Test — with poor internal reliability (.45–.46), in studies with 20–42 people per cell, and the best-powered preregistered replication found nothing. There is no evidence at all that alcohol helps painters, sculptors, designers, or anyone working visually. The single visual-domain experiment found the effect was expectancy, not ethanol. The 17 painters ever asked directly said it helped the freedom and destroyed the discipline.*

# APPENDIX — THE FIVE MYTHS THIS DOSSIER KILLS

1. **PBR won a blue ribbon at the 1893 Chicago World's Fair.** It did not. The fair awarded identical bronze medals to every beer scoring 80%+; there were no gold or silver medals, no grand prize and no ribbons. The blue ribbon had been on Pabst bottles since 1882, and the slogan commemorating the "win" was written in the 1950s. (§1.2)
2. **PBR is a rare, expensive American import in Poland.** From 2020 it was **licence-brewed in Poland by Van Pur at Rakszawa** and sold in Polomarket and Carrefour for about 4 zł. Only in Germany (€8.45/litre) and China ($44/bottle) is it a premium object. (§1.6)
3. **Poland is Europe's drunkest country.** It ranks **8th** in Europe on WHO's total-consumption measure (11.51 L), behind Romania, Latvia, Portugal, Czechia, Bulgaria, Spain and Austria — and its consumption is **falling**, not rising. (§2.6)
4. **Poland banned małpki / raised the małpka tax.** It did neither. The 25 PLN/litre levy from 1 January 2021 is unchanged; the ban was deferred in July 2026 and the 4× increase is a draft for 2027 at the earliest. Alcohol excise rose 5% in January 2026, not 15%, because of a presidential veto on 18 December 2025. (§2.3, §2.5)
5. **"Write drunk, edit sober" — Ernest Hemingway.** Written by **Peter De Vries in 1964**, spoken by a fictional alcoholic poet, symmetrical in the original ("and sometimes I write sober and revise drunk"), and first pinned on Hemingway in a **2005 Usenet post to rec.woodworking**. Hemingway's own documented line: *"Have you ever heard of anyone who drank while he worked? You're thinking of Faulkner."* (§4.5)

**And the sixth, which is the book's thesis:** the study that says drinking makes you more creative used **40 men**, **one verbal word-association task** with an internal reliability of **.45**, and **no placebo** — and when someone ran it properly with 125 people, double-blind and preregistered, the effect was **p = .98**. There is **no** study of alcohol and visual art that found anything. The only 17 painters ever asked said it gave them the freedom and took away the discipline. (§4)
