### THREE CORRECTIONS TO THE BRIEF, UP FRONT

The brief contained three errors. Fixing them now so they do not reach print.

1. **"Mordor's Come Down On Us" is not the English title.** The published English rendering, used by the translator Scotia Gilroy and by *Asymptote* journal, is **_Mordor's Coming to Eat Us: A Secret History of the Slavs_**. Other outlets have used *Mordor Will Come and Eat Us: A Confidential History of the Slavs* (New Eastern Europe, 2014). Both are attested. "Come Down On Us" is not.
   Source: https://www.asymptotejournal.com/nonfiction/ziemowit-szczerek-mordors-coming-to-eat-us-a-secret-history-of-the-slavs/ ; https://neweasterneurope.eu/2014/03/13/schadenfreude/
   **Note:** I found no evidence that a complete English-language book edition exists — only translated excerpts (the second chapter, in *Asymptote*, and material done for the Polish Book Institute's *New Books from Poland* catalogue). Treat "there is an English edition" as [UNVERIFIED].

2. **The novel is "Siódemka" (Siodemka, "The Seven" / "Highway No. 7"), not "Siódmy".** *Siódemka* is the feminine noun for the number seven and here means road **DK7**, the national trunk road. "Siódmy" is the masculine ordinal "seventh" and is not a Szczerek title.
   Source: https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziemowit_Szczerek

3. **"Wściekłe psy Dzikich Pól" is not a Szczerek book.** *Wściekłe psy* is a crime series by **Mieczysław Gorzka**, a different author. Szczerek's crime cycle is about detective **Franciszek "Franc" Kary** and consists of *Cham z kulą w głowie* (Znak, 2020) and *Baciary z piekła rodem* (Znak, 2026).
   Source: https://lubimyczytac.pl/cykl/29920/wsciekle-psy ; https://www.znak.com.pl/p/baciary-z-piekla-rodem-ziemowit-szczerek-455717

# PART ONE — ZIEMOWIT SZCZEREK

## 1. THE BASIC FACTS (verified)

**Ziemowit Cyprian Szczerek**, born **10 April 1978 in Radom**. Journalist, writer, translator. Law degree, Jagiellonian University (Kraków); postgraduate work in political science. Lives between Warsaw and Kraków. Son of Marian Szczerek, a professor of tribology.
Source: https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziemowit_Szczerek (raw wikitext fetched 17.08.2026)

**Radom matters.** He is from the provincial mid-Poland he writes about, not from Warsaw looking at it. This is the single most important biographical fact for the "is he sneering?" question — see §7. He puts Radom into *Siódemka* by name (quote in §7d).

**Affiliations:** *Polityka*, *Nowa Europa Wschodnia*, *New Eastern Europe*, *Gazeta Wyborcza*, *Krytyka Polityczna*, *Tygodnik Powszechny*, *Lampa*, *Ha!art* (standing columnist). Co-hosted the radio show *ZaWschód* on Radiofonia 100.5 FM, Kraków, 2011–2012, with Thymne Chase — a show built around Eastern-bloc cover versions of Western songs. Regular columnist for Halo.Radio from February 2021.
Source: https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziemowit_Szczerek

**The Hunter S. Thompson link is real and specific.** Polish Wikipedia: [VERBATIM] "Jest autorem m.in. pierwszego w Polsce tłumaczenia *Upadku i demoralizacji na Derby Kentucky* Huntera S. Thompsona" — he produced the first Polish translation of Thompson's *The Kentucky Derby Is Decadent and Depraved* (1970), the piece generally credited as the origin of gonzo. He did **not** translate *Fear and Loathing in Las Vegas*; the Polish *Lęk i odraza w Las Vegas* was translated by M. Wróbel and M. Potulny.
Sources: https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziemowit_Szczerek ; https://www.poczytaj.pl/ksiazka/lek-i-odraza-w-las-vegas-hunter-s-thompson,278046

## 4. THE CORE THEMES, IN HIS OWN WORDS

All quotes in this section are **[VERBATIM]** Polish from **Polish Wikiquote** (https://pl.wikiquote.org/wiki/Ziemowit_Szczerek), fetched as raw wikitext on 17.08.2026, with the page numbers Wikiquote gives. Wikiquote is generally reliable for page-cited book quotes but is user-edited: **re-check any quote you intend to print against the printed book.** I flag two probable typos in the wikitext below.

### 4a. Poland as a semi-periphery, and the self-image problem

[VERBATIM] *Międzymorze* (2017), s. 125:
> "My próbujemy przekonać wszystkich, że jesteśmy cywilizowani, że Europa, koronki, że sroce nie spod ogona, pierwsza konstytucja w Europie – a potem udowadniamy coś zupełnie odwrotnego: na przykład cwaniacko zastanawiamy się, jak tę naszą konstytucję, potomkinie pierwszej w Europie, po cichu rozmontować, obejść i w wała zrobić."

Gloss: *We try to convince everyone that we're civilised, that we're Europe, lace cuffs, that we didn't fall out from under a magpie's tail, first constitution in Europe — and then we prove the exact opposite: e.g. we slyly work out how to quietly dismantle that constitution of ours, daughter of the first in Europe, get round it and make a prick of it.*
("Nie sroce spod ogona" is a real fixed Polish idiom meaning roughly "I'm not just any old nobody". "Pierwsza konstytucja w Europie" = the Constitution of 3 May 1791, a permanent Polish boast.)

[VERBATIM] from the PAP interview of 22 June 2025:
> "Sami mieszkamy w kraju, który jest półperyferium. Polska funkcjonuje bowiem pomiędzy trzema cywilizacyjnymi centrami."

Gloss: *We ourselves live in a country that is a semi-periphery. Poland functions between three civilisational centres.*
Source: https://dzieje.pl/rozmaitosci-historyczne/ziemowit-szczerek-jezdze-po-obrzezach-europy-i-ogladam-konce-swiatow (interviewer Anna Kruszyńska, PAP; 22.06.2025). Note: this quote came via a summarising fetch, so treat as [VERBATIM but single-sourced].

**"Półperyferium" / "peryferium do kwadratu"** — semi-periphery, and periphery-squared — are his signature framings of Poland's position. He does not say Poland is the East. He says Poland is the *hinge*, and that Poland's whole psychic economy runs on having someone further east to look down on.

### 4b. "Peryferium do kwadratu" — the DK7 passage (the central Poland-B text)

[VERBATIM] *Siódemka* (2014), s. 23:
> "Siódemka, Siódemka, myślisz, szós polskich królowa, droga, która jest kręgosłupem polskiego państwa, Polski wiślanej, bo ta odrzana to zupełnie inna historia. Tamto to polska kolonia. A tutaj, wzdłuż Siódemki, Siódemeczki, rozwlekła swoje ciało Polska, ta Polska, the Polska, ten projekt, prodżekt, prodżekt Polska, który kilka razy nie wypalił, a teraz jest znów odpalony i terkocze, działa jakoś, pyr-pyr, pyr-pyr, zbiera się jakoś do kupy, próbuje się na nowo określić, a Siódemka jest jego osią przeprowadzoną od Gdańska przez Warszawę po Kraków i góry, przez sam jego środek, rozcina ziemię, z której ta Polska wpływała, które są jej esencją, które nadają jej kształt i ton, bo to Siódemka to polskie centrum, a reszta to peryferia, choć sama Siódemka to peryferium do kwadraty, hej, Siódemka, Siódemka."

Gloss: *Route 7, Route 7, you think, queen of Polish roads, the road that is the spine of the Polish state, of Vistula Poland — because Oder Poland is a completely different story. That's a Polish colony. But here, along the Seven, the little Seven, Poland has sprawled its body, this Poland, the Poland, this project, this prodżekt, prodżekt Poland, which several times failed to fire and is now fired up again and rattling, working somehow, chug-chug, chug-chug, pulling itself together somehow, trying to define itself anew, and the Seven is its axis, drawn from Gdańsk through Warsaw to Kraków and the mountains, straight through its middle, slicing the land this Poland flowed out of, the land that is its essence, that gives it shape and tone — because the Seven is the Polish centre, and the rest is periphery, though the Seven itself is periphery squared. Hey, Siódemka, Siódemka.*

Notes: "szós polskich królowa" inverts the famous Polish schoolroom tag for the Vistula, *"królowa polskich rzek"* (queen of Polish rivers); *szos* = "of roads". "peryferium do **kwadraty**" is almost certainly a Wikiquote transcription slip for *do kwadratu*; **check the book**. "prodżekt" is Polish phonetic spelling of English "project" — a mocking Polglish.

[VERBATIM] *Siódemka*, s. 225:
> "Tu się zaczyna, myślałeś, prawdziwa Polska, płaska, zabudowana gównianymi pustaczanymi pokrakami, ta czarna dziura w środku kraju, ciągnąca się od granic Wielkopolski po Wisłę i od Torunia po Śląsk, ta nędzna i ponura płaskość, która jest polskim krajobrazem bazowym, podstawowym, tytularnym, jak dla Węgier Puszta, dla Słowacji Tatry, dla Francji dolina Mozeli, dla Niemiec skała Lorelei i tak dalej. W Polsce funkcję tę pełni płaski, naleśnikowaty krajobraz pokryty betonowym szajsem bez ładu i składu."

Gloss: *Here begins, you thought, the real Poland — flat, built over with shitty breeze-block monstrosities, this black hole in the middle of the country, running from the edge of Wielkopolska to the Vistula and from Toruń to Silesia, this wretched and dismal flatness which is Poland's base, default, titular landscape, the way the Puszta is for Hungary, the Tatras for Slovakia, the Moselle valley for France, the Lorelei rock for Germany and so on. In Poland the job is done by a flat, pancake-y landscape covered in concrete shit with no rhyme or reason.*
("pustak" = hollow breeze block; "pokraka" = a misshapen freak. "Pustaczany" is his coinage.)

### 4c. The small town — Jędrzejów, the definitive provincial passage

[VERBATIM] *Siódemka*, s. 169:
> "Wjechałeś do Jędrzejowa. Tkanka miejska Jędrzejowa jest, jak wiadomo, Czwartym Cudem Siódemki. (…) Tak więc jędrzejowski rynek był względnie niezabudowanym kawałkiem śmietniska w śmietnisku zabudowanym, które to śmietnisko nosiło ogólną dumną nazwę miasta Jędrzejowa. Budynkiem, który dominował w centrum miasta, był Dom Handlowy Piast, wyglądający tak, jakby go najpierw obsadzili uchodźcy z terenów opanowanych przez Złotą Ordę, a następnie każdy z tych uchodźców otworzył jakiś mały biznesik.
> Jędrzejów był idealnym reprezentantem małomiasteczkowej beznadziei Rzeczpospolitej Polskiej. Miastem o wiele bardziej idealnym niż Zamość, bo precyzyjnie pokazywał, co się stało z miasteczkami w tej części Europy, którą zajmuje państwo o nazwie Polska. To miasto wyglądało na złe. Na wściekłe. Na wściekle wkurwione i wcale mu się nie dziwiłeś. Wkurwione jak sam szatan. I wszyscy jego mieszkańcy byli podkurwieni, bo musieli być podkurwieni."

Gloss: *You drove into Jędrzejów. The urban fabric of Jędrzejów is, as we know, the Fourth Wonder of the Seven. (…) So the Jędrzejów market square was a relatively unbuilt-on piece of rubbish tip inside a built-on rubbish tip, which rubbish tip bore the general proud name of the town of Jędrzejów. The building dominating the town centre was the Piast Department Store, which looked as if it had first been settled by refugees from territory overrun by the Golden Horde, and then each of those refugees had opened some little business.
Jędrzejów was the perfect representative of the small-town hopelessness of the Polish Republic. A far more perfect town than Zamość, because it showed precisely what had happened to little towns in the part of Europe occupied by a state called Poland. This town looked angry. Looked furious. Looked furiously pissed off and you didn't blame it in the least. Pissed off as Satan himself. And all its inhabitants were pissed off, because they had to be pissed off.*

**This is the passage that answers the "does he sneer?" charge either way.** The place is a tip AND the anger is *earned*. Both halves are in the same paragraph.
(Note the "Dom Handlowy" — the PRL-era state department store, now subdivided into dozens of tiny stalls — is a real and universal feature of the Polish small town.)

[VERBATIM] *Siódemka*, s. 55, on Michałowice — the village that outgrew its village-ness and will never reach town-ness:
> "Więc jedziesz przez centrum Michałowic, przez miejsce, które już dawno przerosło swoją wiejskość, ale nigdy nie dochrapie się miejskości, bo nowych miast już się nie tworzy. I nie znajdzie nowego, odpowiadającego swoim ambicjom kształtu, bo nowych kształtów już się nie nadaje. Więc Michałowice zostaną z tym swoim wrzaskiem szyldów w centrum, nadal pozostaną wsią-ulicówką z budowanymi przy Siódemce niby-biurowcami, trochę oszklonymi, trochę spanelowaciałymi, budowanymi bez konsultacji z żadnym urbanistą, zresztą – gdzie urbanista na wsi, panie kochany"

Gloss: *So you drive through the centre of Michałowice, through a place that long ago outgrew its village-ness but will never scrape its way to town-ness, because new towns aren't made any more. And it won't find a new shape to match its ambitions, because new shapes aren't given out any more. So Michałowice will be left with this screaming of shop signs in its centre, will remain a one-street village with pseudo-office-blocks built along the Seven, a bit glazed, a bit gone-to-panelling, built without consulting any town planner — and anyway, where would you find a town planner in a village, my dear sir.*
("Wrzask szyldów" — the *scream of the signage* — is the best phrase in Polish for the post-1989 visual chaos. The formal term for the problem is **"chaos reklamowy"**; the legislative response is the 2015 **"ustawa krajobrazowa"**, the Landscape Act.)

### 4d. Post-communist chaos as an aesthetic — the polbruk line

[VERBATIM] *Siódemka*, s. 30:
> "Wzdłuż klepiska ciągnął się polbruk, zaprawdę, powiadam wam, jeśli kiedykolwiek w Polsce wybuchnie rewolucja, jej symbolem będzie właśnie kostka polbrukowa."

Gloss: *Along the threshing floor ran polbruk paving; verily, I say unto you, if a revolution ever breaks out in Poland its symbol will be the polbruk block.*
**"Polbruk"** (pol-BROOK) is a real brand of concrete interlocking paviour whose name became the generic word for the stuff. It is Poland's national surface: every square, drive, church forecourt and bus stop re-laid in it since 1990, usually in a pink/grey herringbone. If you want ONE object for post-1989 Polish provincial aesthetics, it is a polbruk block. (The joke's other half: the classic revolutionary projectile is a cobblestone.)

[VERBATIM] *Siódemka*, s. 98:
> "Polskie miasta już tak zarosły tym gównem, tym reklamami, tym sajdingiem, tymi dobudówkami, (…) że nie widać już kształtu tej dawnej Polski."
Gloss: *Polish towns have so overgrown with this shit, these adverts, this siding, these lean-tos, that you can't see the shape of the old Poland any more.*
("sajding" = phonetic Polish for vinyl **siding**; "dobudówka" = an unpermitted extension bolted onto a building. Both are exact, usable words.)

### 4e. Poland's relationship to the West — Germany as trauma

[VERBATIM] *Siódemka*, s. 53:
> "Bo Niemcy to największa trauma Polaków, twoich, Paweł, rodaków, to jest wyrzut sumienia stojący za Odrą, który, owszem, jest zdolny do największych możliwych zbrodni, ale który od twojego państwa dzieli taki cywilizacyjny dystans, jak gdyby nie był on od tysiąca lat waszym sąsiadem, tylko znajdował się na obcej planecie. Dlatego gdy tylko wy, Polacy, znajdziecie w Niemczech coś, co nie działa, coś, co jest brudne, coś, co nie jest idealne, jakiekolwiek pęknięcie, to od razu robicie fotki, wrzucacie na Fejsa i cieszycie się jak dzieci – proszę bardzo, im też coś nie wyszło, wcale nie są tacy hop do przodu. Zupełnie jakby każde takie zdjęcie miało być obaleniem mitu o teutońskiej niepokonalności. Małym Grunwaldem."

Gloss: *Because Germany is the greatest trauma of Poles, of your countrymen, Paweł — a pang of conscience standing beyond the Oder, which, yes, is capable of the greatest crimes imaginable, but which is separated from your state by a civilisational distance as if it hadn't been your neighbour for a thousand years but were on an alien planet. That's why the moment you Poles find something in Germany that doesn't work, something dirty, something less than perfect, any crack at all, you immediately take photos, post them on Facebook and rejoice like children — there you go, something didn't work for them either, they're not so far ahead after all. As if every such photo were a refutation of the myth of Teutonic invincibility. A little Grunwald.*
**"Mały Grunwald" — a Little Grunwald — is a superb, usable coinage.** Grunwald (1410) is the Polish-Lithuanian victory over the Teutonic Knights, the most cherished anti-German date in Polish memory.

[VERBATIM] *Siódemka*, s. 166, on Berlin's Kreuzberg:
> "Jeśli naprawdę by chcieli chaosu, to by przyjechali do Polski, a nie siedzieli w tym user-friendly-rebelzone-bullshicie."
Gloss: *If they really wanted chaos they'd come to Poland instead of sitting in this user-friendly-rebelzone-bullshit.*

[VERBATIM] *Siódemka*, s. 164 (character: Tobi):
> "– Polsce, pod pewnym względem, opłaca się znów zostać podbitą. Generalnie zasada jest taka, że o Polsce na świecie dobrze mówi się wyłącznie wtedy, gdy jej nie ma, albo gdy nie jest wolna."
Gloss: *"Poland, in a certain sense, would profit from being conquered again. The general rule is that the world speaks well of Poland only when it doesn't exist, or when it isn't free."*
**This is the self-loathing/self-aggrandisement dialectic in one sentence.**

[VERBATIM] *Siódemka*, s. 244:
> "W każdym normalnym kraju, od tego zacznijmy, nie używa się zwrotu »w każdym normalnym kraju«."
Gloss: *In every normal country, let's start here, nobody uses the phrase "in every normal country."*
**Probably his single most quoted line, and the most useful one in the whole dossier.** "W normalnym kraju…" is a real, ubiquitous Polish conversational tic — the preamble to any complaint about Poland.

### 4f. The East, Slavdom, "turbo-Slavism"

[VERBATIM] *Przyjdzie Mordor i nas zje* (2013), s. 68, spoken by the character Taras Kabat:

### 5c. DRINKING AS THE ENGINE OF THE REPORTAGE — the Vigor chapter

The best primary evidence of how drinking works in Szczerek is the second chapter of *Mordor*, published in full English translation by **Scotia Gilroy** in *Asymptote*. Everything below is **[VERBATIM]** from that published translation.
Source: https://www.asymptotejournal.com/nonfiction/ziemowit-szczerek-mordors-coming-to-eat-us-a-secret-history-of-the-slavs/

The editorial set-up:
> "Just like the beatniks, the narrator and his sidekick Havran are constantly intoxicated. Their alcohol of choice is 'Vigor,' a strong 'balsam' that can be bought over the counter in Ukrainian pharmacies."

The buying ritual — the pharmacist keeps smiling:
> "So I went to the pharmacy to get some more Vigor balsam while Havran headed to a grocery store for some kvass. Just like every time I'd bought Vigor, the pharmacist smiled to herself. It rattled me.
> 'What's so funny about Vigor balsam?' I asked.
> 'Nothing, nothing,' she answered."

Drinking on the steps where Bruno Schulz was shot, beside a memorial candle:
> "I took my two bottles and went back to the bakery steps. Havran was already sitting there. The candle lit by the photographer was burning at his feet. We had a drink and gazed at the Drohobych market square."

The mixer:
> "So we bought some more kvass, mixed it with Vigor and went off with them to look for the Street of Crocodiles."

The Polish literature students, drunk, planning to emigrate to Ukraine:
> "Bożena was already as drunk as a skunk. She'd been running up and down the streets, screaming that she loved Ukraine and wanted to live here forever and ever. … They sat there on the curb, passing the bottle of balsam back and forth, and made a pact that as soon as they finished their studies they'd set off for Drohobych."

And the station bar, where the political conversation happens over beer:
> "Some guys at the station bar unanimously agreed that life had been better back in Soviet times, and you'd have to be blind to fail to see—like it or not—that they were right."
> "'If communism had been taken up by the Germans or, even better, the Swedes,' he continued, 'the whole world would be different. Everyone would think it was the best system that could ever exist.'"
> "'You're wrong, Bohdan,' said the nondescript old-timer, stubbing out his cigarette in a huge, heavy ashtray that could easily be used to smash someone's skull. 'If the Germans or the French had introduced communism to the world, we'd have had to feel inferior to them. But in the Soviet era, it was the Russkies who felt inferior towards us, because we'd always been more civilized than them.'"
> "'And the Estonians more than you. Fuck you all, you cock-sucking nationalists,' said the fatso loudly in Russian."

**THE PAYOFF — the whole book in one exchange.** A Ukrainian girl on the train:
> "'I'll tell you why you come here,' she said, ignoring my question. 'You come here because in other countries they laugh at you. And they think of you the same way as you think about us: as a backward shit-hole you can sneer at. And feel superior towards.'
> …
> 'Because everyone thinks you're impoverished, Eastern trash,' she continued, looking me straight in the eyes with those black holes of hers. 'Not just the Germans, but also the Czechs, even the Slovaks and the Hungarians. You only think the Hungarians are such fucking awesome pals of yours. But in fact they make fun of you just like everyone else. Not to mention the Serbs and the Croats. Even the Lithuanians, buddy. Everyone thinks you're just a slightly different version of Russia. The third world. It's only here that you can be patronizing. **Here you make up for the fact that everywhere else they wipe their asses with you.**'"

**AND THE JOKE THAT UNDOES THE DRINKING.** She then points at the bottle:
> "'Tell me,' said the girl, pointing her chin at the bottle of Vigor balsam I was holding, 'why are you drinking impotence medicine so ostentatiously, in front of everyone?'
> Dumbstruck, I raised the bottle's label to eye level, then realized that this gesture was too humiliating, so I lowered it again, but doing that was even more humiliating because it implied idiotic denial, so I raised it again. And, indeed, in fine print, there was something in Ukrainian about 'erectile dysfunction.'
> 'Holy shit,' I blurted. I remembered how, not long before, we'd been swilling Vigor straight from the bottle in the Lviv market square, thinking the reason why everyone was taking photos of us with their cell phones was because we looked so fucking cool. Now I knew why they were doing it. Two brawny Poles publicly tanking up on medicine for erectile dysfunction—it must have been a grand sight."

The last line of the chapter, handing the bottle to the humiliated girls:
> "'Here,' I said, handing them the Vigor. 'Drink up.'"

**This is the single most useful passage in the dossier for a book about drinking.** The structure is: drink → feel like an outlaw → discover the drink is a joke on you → keep drinking anyway. It is the Polish self-image in miniature, and Szczerek built it out of an alcohol.

Note on the drink itself: **[UNVERIFIED as a real product]** — I have not independently confirmed that a Ukrainian pharmacy "balsam" branded *Vigor*, indicated for erectile dysfunction, existed. Treat "Vigor" as an element of the text, not a documented fact. What *is* well documented and safe to invoke generally is the post-Soviet practice of drinking cheap ethanol-based pharmacy tinctures and balsams as alcohol.

The translator's own note, [VERBATIM] — worth having because it names the difficulty of the register:
> "Szczerek is one of the most challenging writers I've translated so far in my career. Firstly, translating vulgar slang so that it has the same explosive power as in the original text is a fine art."
And her closing image:
> "…giving an English-language existence to these two horny, irritable, punk-like Polish men was as exciting as taking a long swig from a bottle of Vigor."

**"Chlać"** [chlac] = to swill, to booze — coarse. **"Chlanie"** = the noun. This is the register Szczerek uses; it is not neutral, it is deliberately ugly. The neutral verb is *pić* (to drink); *chlać* is what you say about someone you disapprove of, or about yourself when you are being honest.

[VERBATIM] *Siódemka*, s. 8:

### 5f. Where drinking sits in his method, summarised

[PARA, my synthesis of the sources above] Four functions:
1. **Access.** The drink is the entry ticket to a conversation with a stranger — the station bar in Drohobych; the friend "przy piwie" in Budapest.
2. **Licence.** Being drunk licenses the narrator's cruelty and lets him disclaim it afterwards.
3. **Subject.** The drinking IS the reportage. There is no sober observation to fall back on.
4. **The joke on himself.** The Vigor reveal: the substance that made him feel like an outlaw turns out to make him ridiculous. This is what separates Szczerek from an imitator — he arranges his own humiliation.

Reviewers in Poland notice all four. [PARA] One review notes that essentially every conversation in *Mordor* happens at a table loaded with alcohol; that the Polish backpackers explicitly want to drink with locals because "everyone knows" Russians drink always and everywhere and never refuse free vodka; and that Szczerek openly admits he was drunk and on various substances while observing.
Sources: https://charliethelibrarian.com/ziemowit-szczerek-przyjdzie-mordor-i-nas-zje-czyli-tajna-historia-slowian/ ; http://dofi.com.pl/przyjdzie-mordor-i-nas-zje-czyli-tajna-historia-slowian-ziemowit-szczerek/

## 6. THE POLITICS: "POLAND B", THE LEFT, AND THE PROVINCE

Two Krytyka Polityczna essays are the key texts.

**"Szczerek: Za co jestem wdzięczny PiS-owi?"** (What am I grateful to PiS for?), 09.08.2019 — https://krytykapolityczna.pl/kraj/szczerek-za-co-jestem-wdzieczny-pis-owi/

[VERBATIM] (via Wikiquote, which cites this article):
> "Nawet jeśli PKB w Kongresówce było większe niż w pruskiej Wielkopolsce, to w Wielkopolsce żyło się przeciętnemu człowiekowi lepiej. Prusy rozwijały prowincję, a w Rosji bogactwo kumulowało się w rękach wąskiej klasy posiadającej, podczas gdy jej prowincja żyła jak w chałupach z czasów Mieszka. Tak więc mógłbym się nawet podpisać się pod słowami Jarosława Kaczyńskiego, który na spotkaniu z wyborcami gdzieś na Pomorzu mówił o tym, że »urealnienie gospodarki i pieniądza« za czasów Balcerowicza było potrzebne, ale zapomniano wówczas o ludziach, traktując ich jako cyfry na drodze do sukcesu. I o tym, że liberalizm ma swoje miejsce w historii myśli ekonomicznej, ale w Polsce przyjął on mocno karykaturalną formę."

Gloss: *Even if GDP in Congress Poland was higher than in Prussian Wielkopolska, the average person lived better in Wielkopolska. Prussia developed the provinces, whereas in Russia wealth accumulated in the hands of a narrow propertied class while its provinces lived in huts from the days of Mieszko. So I could even put my name to Jarosław Kaczyński's words, said at a voters' meeting somewhere in Pomerania: that the "making real of the economy and of money" under Balcerowicz was necessary, but that people were forgotten at the time, treated as digits on the road to success. And that liberalism has its place in the history of economic thought, but in Poland it took a strongly caricatural form.*

**This is the load-bearing point for "Poland B".** The A/B division (**"Polska A i Polska B"** — a real, standard Polish term: roughly the richer west/north-west vs the poorer east/south-east) is treated by Szczerek not as a slur but as the durable footprint of the **partitions** (*zabory*): Prussian, Austrian and Russian Poland left different infrastructures and habits, still visible in railway maps and election maps to this day. He then makes the transition point: the 1989–90 **Balcerowicz Plan** shock therapy was necessary, and its human cost was ignored.
**[UNVERIFIED]** — the origin of the phrase "Polska A / Polska B". It is universally used in Polish media; I did not verify who coined it or when. Poles now extend the joke to **"Polska C"**. Do not attribute the coinage to anyone.

**"Szczerek: Wytrawny koktajl z polskości"** (A dry cocktail of Polishness), 17.12.2019 — https://krytykapolityczna.pl/kraj/szczerek-wytrawny-koktajl-z-polskosci/

[PARA, from a summarising fetch — **re-read before quoting**] The argument: Poland is commonly held to be ugly and unloved, but there is a style hidden inside it waiting to be found. The Polish left occupies an intellectual-critical position and holds itself at a distance from Polish reality; the right, however toxically, shows genuine *tenderness* toward the Polish landscape and Polish culture. He urges the left to learn to see value in **"prostactwo"** (boorishness, crudity) and in the bottom-up forms of Polish culture rather than rejecting them wholesale, and to build an alternative narrative of Polishness that is not solely critical.

**The title is a drinking metaphor and worth unpacking.** *Wytrawny* means "dry" of a wine, and also "seasoned, accomplished" of a person. A *koktajl z polskości* is a cocktail made *of Polishness*. The essay's turn — that even a landscape **"jabol"** can be refined into something better — uses a real and important piece of Polish drinking vocabulary: *jabol* is cheap, sweet, fortified fruit "wine", the emblematic bad drink of the late PRL and the 1990s park bench. See Part Two.

**On Kraków as a hiding place.** The *Siódemka* line at §5e — Kraków as "one of those few places in Poland where you can hide from Poland" — is the other half of the Poland-A/B argument. The metropolitan liberal does not confront the province; he *escapes* into a handful of protected cities.

### 7d. The defence

[PARA] The counter-case, from Polish reviewers: *Siódemka* "only seemingly mocks the province" — *"tylko pozornie kpi z prowincji"* — because the province is precisely where the truth about the country can be learned; Szczerek circulates through the peripheries watching a sense of injustice grow in ordinary people, and tries not merely to mock their fears but to understand them; and he is aware that his own ideological camp helped awaken the dreams of national power it now deplores.
Sources: https://culture.pl/pl/dzielo/ziemowit-szczerek-siodemka ; https://krytykapolityczna.pl/kraj/szczerek-wytrawny-koktajl-z-polskosci/

And the biographical defence: **he is from Radom**, and he puts the Radom tower block in the book by name:

[VERBATIM] *Siódemka*, s. 156:
> "Nie mogłeś sobie wyobrazić, wychodząc z rodzinnego bloku w Radomiu, mieście leżącym w obrębie środkowopolskiej Pustaci Wewnątrzobwarzankowej, że świat, który jest inny od tego dookoła, może realnie istnieć. (…) Okej, wiedziałeś, że istnieją Niemcy. Niemcy są dla każdego Polaka czymś w rodzaju na sto procent istniejącego nieba i piekła jednocześnie. Z jakiegoś powodu byłeś też pewien, że istnieje Rosja, która była tym samym, co Niemcy, tylko na odwrót. Ale wszystko inne było tylko baśnią z książek, kaset wideo i kolorowych czasopism."

Gloss: *Walking out of your family tower block in Radom, a town lying inside the Central Polish Inner-Bagel Wasteland, you couldn't imagine that a world different from the one around you could really exist. (…) Okay, you knew Germany existed. For every Pole, Germany is a hundred-percent-existing heaven and hell at once. For some reason you were also sure Russia existed, which was the same as Germany only in reverse. But everything else was just a fairy tale out of books, video cassettes and glossy magazines.*
**"Pustać Wewnątrzobwarzankowa"** — the *Inner-Obwarzanek Wasteland* — is his own coinage and a very good joke. An **obwarzanek** is the ring-shaped Kraków bread; *"gmina obwarzankowa"* / *"Polska obwarzankowa"* is real Polish administrative jargon for the doughnut-ring commune that surrounds a city without including it. He is calling the middle of Poland the hole in the middle of the bagel.
**"Blok"** = a prefab concrete apartment block; **"blokowisko"** = a whole estate of them; **"z bloków"** = "from the blocks", a social marker. Essential vocabulary.

## 9b. SUPPLEMENT — THE PUBLISHED ENGLISH RENDERINGS (culture.pl)

Source for this whole section: **Mikołaj Gliński, "Ziemowit Szczerek – Biography", Culture.pl, 5 Sept 2013, updated by AP July 2019** — https://culture.pl/en/artist/ziemowit-szczerek (fetched via curl on 17.08.2026; the page 403s to ordinary fetchers). Culture.pl is run by the **Adam Mickiewicz Institute**, a Polish state cultural body — i.e. this is Poland's own official English-language presentation of him, which makes it useful as an authorised gloss.

### THE KEROUAC/BENZEDRINE PASSAGE — the single clearest statement of drinking as method
[VERBATIM, culture.pl's published English translation of Szczerek]:
> "Instead of Benzedrine, we had Vigor Balm (a type of herbal alcohol). Instead of rural USA and Mexico, we had Ukraine. Our goal was the same. We took our backpacks and hit the road. We didn't read Kerouac, because he was impossible to read. It was so full of pulsating and entangled guts. But also we felt stupid because, contrary to Kerouac, we had no real purpose. Kerouac and the others did do some kind of revolution, and we only ran like fucking hell through doors already open."

**This is the thesis.** The Beats had amphetamine and a revolution; the Poles had cheap herbal booze and a set of doors that were already open. **"We only ran like fucking hell through doors already open"** is the line about the whole post-1989 generation. Pair it with the *Mordor* s. 41 quote at §5d — the ersatz purpose — and you have the argument complete.

### On Ukraine as an intensified Poland
[VERBATIM, culture.pl paraphrasing then quoting]: Western Ukraine is the place that most resembles Poland — "it's Poland, only with higher dosage of the East or, as the author puts it, **'it's Poland; only it pisses you off more'**."

### On Ukraine as the site of childhood memory
[VERBATIM, culture.pl's translation of Szczerek]:
> "When I first came to Ukraine, I immediately associated it with my childhood and holidays in the countryside. Everything was mysterious, like grandma's attic. The warped colors, gestures and voices – everything amped up to the max."

**Important for the "does he sneer?" question.** His stated first response to Ukraine is not contempt but *recognition* — it is his own childhood, louder.

### On Radom, his home town, and Poland's signage
[VERBATIM, culture.pl's translation from *Paczka radomskich*, 2010]:
> "Heaviness, crudeness, dirt, and here and there a spaceship from another planet: a gas station, exhaling the delicate perfume of violets, open and closed with a silent rustle, a popcorn-scented multiplex with cloud-soft carpets, a shopping center with H&Ms and mango shakes at McDonald's. And then you have to go out again and see those signboards painted with miscellaneous shit, to see this Polish attempt at adapting to its surroundings; time to admit that the attempt is unsuccessful."

Culture.pl's editorial note flags that the "signboards painted with miscellaneous shit" clause is [VERBATIM] "a truly delightful adaptation of a Mickiewicz line which translation does not render". [PARTLY UNVERIFIED — I could not identify which Mickiewicz line.] **If you use this passage, find the Polish original; the joke is in the original, not the English.**

And the killer editorial line about Radom, [VERBATIM from culture.pl]:
> "Writing about Radom, Szczerek notices that at a deeper level, it does not differ much from Warsaw. To those who wish to know where Radom ends and where Warsaw (**the Radom of Europe**) begins, we suggest reading the text 'Radom Against Poland'. **Essentially, Radom never ends.**"

**"Warsaw, the Radom of Europe" and "Radom never ends" — this is Poland-B recursion in two phrases.** Note the parallel with "peryferium do kwadratu" (§4b).

### On the Zamarstyniv district of Lviv — the aesthetics of post-Soviet chaos
[VERBATIM, culture.pl's translations of Szczerek]:
> streets: "an incredible cocktail of rubble, dry mud and stones stubbornly pretending to be a sidewalk"
> neighbourhoods: "bundles of wires hung like vines, heaps of rusting scrap and virtually everything else, lying around helter-skelter in the courtyards, all resembling something organic, something that would soon sprout branches"
> and the narrator's summary: "form and aesthetics have been discarded, as a useless fad..."

Culture.pl's own gloss of the post-Soviet space, [VERBATIM]:
> "It is a space where nothing resembles anything, or rather everything resembles nothing, where objects pretend to be their counterparts (mainly Western ones) rather than being themselves – and all of this is weird, seemingly unfinished as if deconstructed from the very beginning."

**"Objects pretend to be their counterparts rather than being themselves"** is the cleanest formulation of Szczerek's aesthetic of post-communist chaos anywhere in this dossier — and it is the same idea as the Bar Smakosz that "looked the way a Bar Smakosz on a national road can look" (§5e).

[VERBATIM, culture.pl's translations]:

### On *Rzeczpospolita zwycięska* — the alternate-history logic, and why it matters for self-image
[VERBATIM, culture.pl's gloss]:
> "The myth of powerful Poland – alongside with the myths of noble Poland and tolerant Poland – is probably one of the strongest elements in historical narratives and, unlike the others mentioned, actually influences Polish politics and self-perception. Szczerek asks: what if these dreams and fantasies became real? And he answers that such a powerful Poland would soon become a threat not only to itself but also the world – and in the end that **this imaginary Poland becomes surprisingly similar to the real one, with all its complexes and problems**."
> "But this caricature appeals to Poles, and they dream of this imagined yet powerful Poland. Thus **Szczerek's caricature becomes catharsis**."

**Three named Polish self-myths, from an official Polish source: powerful Poland, noble Poland, tolerant Poland.** That is a usable taxonomy.

### On *Siódemka* — Wojciech Orliński's framing in *Gazeta Wyborcza*
[VERBATIM, quoted in culture.pl]:
> "Szczerek references Heart of Darkness, not the original version by Conrad, but Francis Ford Coppola's film. The closer we are to colonel Kurtz's abode – Warsaw and Lordzisko hotel – the more macabre we find."

And culture.pl's own description of the road's inventory, [VERBATIM]: *"a huge, grey void, 'Polish ugliness', architectural nightmares, ruined towns, Sarmatian names"*, mixed with post-apocalyptic visions taken from *The Witcher*.
**"Sarmatian"** [sarmacki] is essential Polish vocabulary: **Sarmatism** (*sarmatyzm*) is the noble ideology of the old Commonwealth — the myth that the szlachta descended from the ancient Sarmatians — and today the word names the whole complex of Polish boastfulness, provincialism, hospitality-to-excess and heavy drinking. **If you want the historical name for the drinking culture Szczerek describes, it is Sarmatism.**

### On *Tatuaż z tryzubem* — Ukraine as a "theoretical country"
[VERBATIM, culture.pl]: the vision of Ukraine as a **"theoretical country"**, which never had the chance to truly exist, where **"everything looks as if there had been a nuclear holocaust"**, and where the **"apocalypse […] already took place and now everything slowly, calmly deteriorated"**.
(The Polish review's title for the same idea was *"Kraj teoretyczny"* — Krzysztof Cieślik, *Polityka*, 01.12.2015, https://www.polityka.pl/tygodnikpolityka/kultura/ksiazki/1642111,1,recenzja-ksiazki-ziemowit-szczerek-tatuaz-z-tryzubem.read . Cieślik's praise, [VERBATIM]: *"opowiada historie, których nie znamy (...) i jak zawsze robi to z biglem i efektowną, mięsistą frazą"* — he tells stories we don't know, and as always does it with verve and a showy, meaty phrase. **"Mięsista fraza" — a meaty phrase — is the standard Polish critical compliment for his prose.**)

### On borders
[VERBATIM, Szczerek in an interview for his publisher Ha!art, quoted by culture.pl]:
> "The physical borders of countries are only the outside layout. Life throbs underneath."

## 9d. SUPPLEMENT — "WHERE POLAND IS AT ITS MOST POLISH" (culture.pl on *Siódemka*)

Source: **Mikołaj Gliński, "Where Poland Is at its Most Polish", Culture.pl, May 2015** — https://culture.pl/en/article/where-poland-is-at-its-most-polish (fetched via curl 17.08.2026; 403s to ordinary fetchers). Again: Adam Mickiewicz Institute, i.e. Poland's official English cultural voice.

**This is the single most important text in the dossier for the "is he sneering at provincial Poles?" question, because it shows the book arguing the opposite.**

### Drinking is explicitly named as the coping mechanism
[VERBATIM]:
> "This man-made landscape includes the most bizarre architecture with hotels, side-road bars and gas stations, little shabby towns with the strangest urban lay-outs. In the novel this ugliness is hardly bearable, and **the characters must cope with it with the use of drugs and alcohol, which turns this voyage into a psychedelic trip where the ultimate goal is gaining the almost mystical understanding of what Poland really is.**"

**Intoxication is not decoration here. It is the stated instrument of national self-knowledge.** That is the whole gonzo claim in one sentence, and it is being made by a Polish state cultural institute about a Polish novel.

### The equation: Poland = chaos + being pissed off
[VERBATIM, a character in the novel]:
> "Nothing has a shape here. Here it's chaos. Satan. Once this city had a shape, and now it's chaos. It's impossible to live in chaos. Constantly pissed off."

And Gliński's gloss, [VERBATIM]:
> "This lack of shape becomes a key element of the Szczerek's definition of Poland. In fact, **this chaos is the shape of Poland (more precisely, the equation is: Poland = chaos + being pissed off)**."

### The road's mythology
[VERBATIM, Szczerek in an interview, quoted by Gliński]:

[VERBATIM, Gliński]: *"In fact the road to Polish independence starts on Siódemka: In August 1914 it was along its path that the forces led by **Józef Piłsudski** marched from Kraków towards the Russian-Austrian border"*.

### THE ARGUMENT FOR THE DEFENCE — he wants Poles to stop being ashamed
This is the passage to keep. [VERBATIM, Gliński summarising and quoting]:
> "Rather than criticizing this Polish mish-mash, Szczerek uses it as a means to interpret the Polish identity and history - and in the end manages to accept it as expression of Polish culture."

> "In an interview, he suggested that rather than being embarrassed about this Polish space, and making efforts to hide it and dodge it, which only mimicks the real effort of modernization, one should maybe start taking advantage of it, consciously build on it, and turn it into Polish trademark."

> "**Every country creates icons out of its public space. Americans keep on showing off their roadside diners, those boring suburbs, those New York fire escapes. Shouldn't Poland do the same?**"

And Gliński's conclusion, [VERBATIM]:
> "Why not make the landscape of Siódemka Poland's trademark? … Why not build on it, make it more sophisticated? **If Poland's trademark has to be the ubiquitous roadside signboards and advertising chaos, why not make them nice signboards and elaborate chaos?**"
> "**Siódemka is definitely not Route 66, but maybe it could be more like the Las Vegas Strip.**"

**"Elaborate chaos" is the position.** It is the same argument as *"Wytrawny koktajl z polskości"* (§6) — refine the *jabol*, don't pour it away. **Anyone claiming Szczerek simply sneers at provincial Poland has to get past this.**

### The "aesthetic terrorists" and Filip Springer — a real book inside a novel
[VERBATIM]:
> "One of them features a quasi-guerilla troop made up of young urban hipsters who travel around Poland dispatching terrorist attacks on buildings that they consider ugly. **These aesthetic terrorists are keen readers of Filip Springer's book *Wanna z kolumnadą* – a real book and bitter critique of Polish aesthetic tastes in architecture and public space.**"

## 10. THE FIVE THINGS MOST LIKELY TO EMBARRASS THE BOOK — read this first

1. **Do NOT write "na trzeciego" as a Polish idiom for the three-man bottle share.** It could not be verified in any Polish dictionary. The ritual is documented as **Russian/Soviet** (*сообразить на троих*). See §15.
2. **Do NOT confuse *ogórek kiszony* (brine-fermented, sour — the vodka one) with *ogórek konserwowy* (vinegar-pickled, sweetish).** They are different foods and no Pole confuses them. See §17.
3. **Do NOT call *Żubrówka* a "wódka czysta"** — it is *gatunkowa* (flavoured) — **and do not claim the blade of grass flavours it.** Polish Wikipedia says it is purely decorative. See §16.
4. **Do NOT use *meta* to mean "a drinking session."** It means a **place**. And to a modern young Polish reader it also means **methamphetamine**. See §12.
5. **Do NOT assert "the Polish shot is 50 ml", the eye-contact-while-clinking rule, or a vodka called "Średzka".** None verified; the last appears not to exist.

**And the three best-verified images to actually use:** the *kusztyk* glass with no base, which cannot be set down until it is empty (§18); pouring **«по булькам»**, dividing one bottle three ways by counting the glugs (§15); and the PRL *monopolowy* that opened at 10:00 and was legally forbidden to sell anything until 13:00 (§14).

## 11. "NA ZDROWIE" AND "STO LAT"

### na zdrowie
- Spelling: **na zdrowie** (no diacritics). ASCII: na zdrowie. Literal: "to health".
- Register: neutral, universal, printable anywhere.
- **VERIFIED — it has two distinct functions, and Wikisłownik lists them as separate senses:**
  - [VERBATIM] *"zwrot używany w stosunku do kogoś, kto właśnie kichnął"* — a phrase used towards someone who has just sneezed.
  - [VERBATIM] *"zwrot używany przed toastem"* — a phrase used before a toast. Example given: *"Wypijmy na zdrowie Wikisłownika!"*
  - Source: https://pl.wiktionary.org/wiki/na_zdrowie
- **So the same two words cover a sneeze and a shot of vodka. That is true, it is checkable, and it is a gift.**
- Historical depth [VERIFIED]: Muzeum Polskiej Wódki records that in the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth toasts were called ***wiwaty*** or ***zdrowia*** — the latter name tied to alcohol having been treated as medicine. Each was delivered standing, and the honoured person was greeted with a collective ***"Niech żyje!"*** ("Long may he live!"). https://muzeumpolskiejwodki.pl/aktualnosci/na-pierwsza-nozke-podstawy-polskich-toastow/

### sto lat
- Spelling: **sto lat**. ASCII: sto lat. Literal: "a hundred years".
- **As a phrase** — Wikisłownik [VERBATIM]: *"życzenia pomyślnego i długiego życia w dobrej kondycji składane najczęściej z okazji urodzin, rocznic i uroczystych wydarzeń"* — wishes of a prosperous and long life in good health, most often on birthdays, anniversaries and ceremonial occasions. https://pl.wiktionary.org/wiki/sto_lat
- **As a song** — Polish Wikipedia gives the opening [VERBATIM]: *"Sto lat, sto lat, / Niechaj żyje, żyje nam"*. Described as a *"tradycyjna polska piosenka śpiewana z okazji składania życzeń"*. Crucially [VERBATIM]: *"Autor słów, jak i melodii, a także pochodzenie nie są ustalone"* — **the author of both words and melody, and the origin, are not established.** https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sto_lat
- **When it is actually used [VERIFIED]:** at *"bardzo nieformalnych okazji, zwykle urodzinach czy imieninach"* (very informal occasions, usually birthdays or **name-days**) but **also at formal state occasions** — sung at President Lech Kaczyński's inauguration and during a John Paul II visit. Wikipedia compares it to "Happy Birthday to You" and "For He's a Jolly Good Fellow".
- **WRITING CAUTION.** *Sto lat* is a **wish and a song**, not a clinking toast. **Three men passing a bottle on a bench do not sing *Sto lat*. They say *na zdrowie*.** The full commonly-sung lyric with the *"Jeszcze raz, jeszcze raz…"* repeat is **[UNVERIFIED beyond the two lines quoted above]** — do not print further lines.
- **Also note "imieniny" (name-day).** In Poland the name-day has historically outranked the birthday as the drinking occasion; the person celebrating is the ***solenizant***. That word is verified in the toast-order source at §18.

## 12. "META" — CHECKED HARD. IT IS A PLACE, NOT A SESSION.

- Spelling: **meta**. ASCII: meta. Standard sense: the finish line of a race.

**Wikisłownik senses, with register labels [VERBATIM]:**
- (1.1) *"sport. miejsce, które należy osiągnąć w zawodach"* — finish line
- (1.2) **pot.** *"melina, nielegalny punkt sprzedaży alkoholu"* — **a den; an illegal alcohol sales point**
- (1.3) **pot.** *"miejsce, w którym można się zatrzymać, zamieszkać lub spędzić miło czas"* — a place to stop, stay, or spend time pleasantly
- (1.4) **pot.** *"kryjówka dla kogoś ukrywającego się, będącego w kolizji z prawem"* — a hideout for someone on the run
- (1.6) **slang.** *"metamfetamina"*
Source: https://pl.wiktionary.org/wiki/meta

**sjp.pl [VERBATIM]:** 1. *"dokładnie zaznaczone miejsce kończące wyścig; finisz"*; 2. **środowiskowo** (in-group usage): *"miejsce ukrywania się i spotkań przestępców"*; 3. **dawniej** (formerly): *"odległość, dystans"* — surviving only in *na krótką metę* / *na dłuższą metę* (in the short/long run). https://sjp.pl/meta

**Miejski.pl [VERBATIM] user definitions and examples:**
- *"Miejsce gdzie prowadzona jest nielegalna sprzedaż papierosów, narkotyków lub wódki."*
- *"Meta jest to wódka niewiadomego pochodzenia, powodująca ślepnięcie, cena za 0.5l to ok. 7 zł."*
- *"– Mam 3 zł / – Idziem na metę, będzie rama fajów."*
- *"– Co dzisiaj pijemy? / – Metę."*
Source: https://www.miejski.pl/slowo-Meta
**slang.pl [VERBATIM]:** *"Miejsce sprzedaży nielegalnego alkoholu"* — typically run out of a private flat from unregistered production. https://slang.pl/meta/

### The verdict on "meta"
1. **Primarily a PLACE** — the flat, house or kiosk selling alcohol illegally, especially after hours.
2. **It also means a hideout or crash pad.** This is the sense that lets a character say *"idziemy na metę"* meaning "let's go back to mine".
3. **By metonymy it can name the booze itself** — *"wódka niewiadomego pochodzenia"*, vodka of unknown origin. That is user-submitted street usage, not dictionary usage; mark it as such.
4. **It does NOT mean "a drinking session."** No source supports this. **For a session use *libacja*, *melanż*, *popijawa* or *biba* (§19). Using *meta* for a session is a printable error.**
5. **The modern collision:** *meta* also = methamphetamine (and = Metallica). In a PRL setting the alcohol sense is safe; in a 2020s setting it is ambiguous.

### melina — the better-documented sibling
- Wikisłownik [VERBATIM]: (1.1) **pot.** *"kryjówka przestępców"*; (1.2) **pot.** *"lokal prywatny, w którym nielegalnie handluje się alkoholem lub narkotykami"*; (1.3) **pogard.** (contemptuous) *"podrzędny bar, w którym gromadzi się margines społeczny"*. https://pl.wiktionary.org/wiki/melina
- A 2026 Polish press piece on Warsaw's night ban [VERBATIM]: *"Melina to zjawisko znane z czasów PRL. Polega na otwarciu punktu sprzedającego alkohol nielegalnie."* — the melina is a phenomenon known from PRL times; it consists of opening a point selling alcohol illegally. https://nczas.info/2026/07/13/wesola-prohibicja-jak-glupie-przepisy-zamienily-warszawe-w-miasto-pelne-melin-i-pogotowiarzy/
- **[UNVERIFIED: that "meta" specifically was coined in the PRL.]** The *phenomenon* is PRL-documented; the word's dating is not.
- **Modern echo, verified and vivid:** Warsaw's night ban has produced ***pogotowiarze*** — night alcohol couriers. [VERBATIM] *"To usługa polegająca na możliwości przywiezienia do domu klienta butelek alkoholu – właśnie w godzinach nocnych"*, at *"za butelkę piwa z dostawą zapłacić trzeba od 11 do 25 złotych, za wino od 50 do 120"* — 11–25 zł for a delivered beer, 50–120 zł for wine. Same nczas.info URL. **The *meta* did not die; it acquired a phone number.**
  > ⚠ **CORRECTION — SEE §33 AND §35.3.** The "melinas came back" framing (from an opinion site) is **contradicted by the state's own evaluation.** KCPU/IPiN's 442-page study found, [VERBATIM]: *"**Nie odnotowano powstawania tzw. melin, którymi »straszyli« przedstawiciele przedsiębiorców.**"* — **no *melinas* appeared; that was industry scaremongering.** What the researchers did document was cross-border shopping (*"turystyka alkoholowa"*) and online ordering. **The delivery couriers are real; "the melina came back" is not.**

## 13. "KAC", "KAC VEGAS", "KLIN KLINEM"

### kac
- Spelling: **kac**. ASCII: kac.
- Wikisłownik: unpleasant symptoms (headache, thirst) from excessive alcohol. **Etymology: from German *Katzenjammer*.** Collocation listed: ***kac moralny*** — a "moral hangover", remorse after doing something shameful, **not necessarily drink-related at all**. Synonyms: *kociokwik*, *syndrom dnia wczorajszego*, *glątwa*. Phrase: ***na kacu*** ("hungover"). https://pl.wiktionary.org/wiki/kac
- Register: fully standard, printable in a newspaper.
- ***Kac moralny* is the one worth stealing.** A whole country can have one.

### kac gigant
- Spelling: **kac gigant**. ASCII: kac gigant. Literally "giant hangover".
- **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED]** Not a headword in Wikisłownik, sjp.pl or wsjp.pl; the Miejski.pl page 404s. It is in live use (e.g. a song title, https://groove.pl/whitegrizzly/kac_gigant/piosenka/). **Treat it as a transparent noun+adjective collocation, not a fixed idiom.** Safe for a character to say. **Do not gloss it as "a Polish idiom."**

### Kac Vegas — CONFIRMED, and the sequels are the joke
- Polish Wikipedia: Polish title ***Kac Vegas***, original *The Hangover*, 2009, dir. Todd Phillips. https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kac_Vegas
- **The sequels [VERIFIED]:** part II is ***Kac Vegas w Bangkoku*** — "Hangover Vegas in Bangkok". The distributor had baked "Vegas" into the brand and then had to send the film to Thailand. Part III is ***Kac Vegas III***.
- **This is a genuinely funny, genuinely verified piece of Polish popular culture** and it is the reason "kac" is now attached to "Vegas" in the Polish ear.

### klin / klin klinem — the exact phrases
- **The dictionary lemma** — wsjp.pl (PAN) gives ***ktoś wybija klin klinem*** (perf. *wybił*), defined [VERBATIM]: *"ktoś radzi sobie w niekorzystnej sytuacji, stosując w tym celu środki takie same jak te"* — someone copes with an unfavourable situation using the same means that caused it. wsjp notes it *"often appears in shortened form as **klin klinem**"*. Origin given as Latin ***clavum clavo pellere***. https://wsjp.pl/haslo/podglad/28244/ktos-wybija-klin-klinem
- **So the exact phrases are: *klin klinem* (everyday) and *wybijać / wybić klin klinem* (full).** A man reaching for the morning beer says **"Klin klinem!"**
- **IMPORTANT: *klin klinem* is general-purpose, not exclusively alcoholic.** wsjp's corpus examples include curing loneliness and depression. The specifically alcoholic sense sits on the noun ***klin*** alone.
- **klin = the drink itself** — Miejski.pl [VERBATIM]: *"Potoczna nazwa jednego z najpopularniejszych środków na porannego kaca, czyli seta wódki (lub innego mocnego alkoholu) zamiast kefiru."* — the colloquial name for one of the most popular morning-hangover remedies: **a *seta* of vodka (or other spirit) instead of kefir.** Example [VERBATIM]: *"Sławek chodź walniem klina bo nie wyrobie!"* https://www.miejski.pl/slowo-Klin
- **Note the kefir.** It is in the source and it is a real Polish touch: **kefir is the sober man's hangover cure; the *klin* is the drinker's.** So is *ogórek kiszony* brine — but see the caution at §17.
- Register: *klin* in this sense is colloquial/street; *klin klinem* is universally understood and printable.

### "pod sklepem" — the scene, and the law that creates it
- **The phrase itself is transparent** (*pod sklepem* = "outside / in front of the shop"). **[UNVERIFIED as a fixed idiom]** — no dictionary entry lexicalises it. **Do not present it as an idiom.** It is plain description that happens to name a recognised social scene.
- **What IS verified is the law.** Art. 14 of the *ustawa o wychowaniu w trzeźwości i przeciwdziałaniu alkoholizmowi* (Act on Upbringing in Sobriety and Counteracting Alcoholism), [VERBATIM]: *"Zabrania się spożywania napojów alkoholowych w miejscu publicznym, z wyjątkiem miejsc przeznaczonych do ich spożycia na miejscu, w punktach sprzedaży tych napojów."* — drinking alcohol in a public place is forbidden, except at places designated for on-premises consumption at points of sale. Councils may make exceptions by resolution. https://lexlege.pl/ustawa-o-wychowaniu-w-trzezwosci-i-przeciwdzialaniu-alkoholizmowi/art-14/
- **Novelistic consequence, legally exact:** the men at the shop wall are committing an offence and they know it. The bottle goes in the bag; the cup goes behind the back. **Same reflex as the Soviet ritual in §15.**

### sklep monopolowy — and the best PRL detail in the dossier
- Spelling: **sklep monopolowy**; the adjective alone works as a noun in speech — *"idę do monopolowego"*. ASCII: sklep monopolowy / monopolowy.
- Polish Wikipedia [VERBATIM]: *"Placówka handlowa zajmująca się wyłącznie sprzedażą napojów alkoholowych"* — an outlet dealing **exclusively** in alcoholic drinks. The name comes from trading in goods controlled by the ***Państwowy Monopol Spirytusowy*** (State Spirit Monopoly). https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sklep_monopolowy
- The **State Spirit Monopoly was established in 1924** as a fiscal instrument of the Second Polish Republic. https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Państwowy_Monopol_Spirytusowy
- **THE DETAIL [VERIFIED]:** in the **1980s** a prohibition-style rule **banned alcohol sales before 13:00**, and yet monopolowy shops **typically opened at 10:00** — three dead hours in which a shop that sold nothing but alcohol could sell nothing at all. https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sklep_monopolowy
  **A shop open for three hours a day with nothing it is allowed to sell. That is the whole PRL in one shopfront.**
- After 1989 the extreme specialisation largely vanished; *monopolowy* now informally means any shop where alcohol is the main line.

### Żabka
- Spelling: **Żabka**. ASCII: Zabka. Literally "little frog".
- Polish Wikipedia [VERBATIM]: *"polska sieć sklepów convenience (małoformatowych) działających na zasadzie franczyzy"* — founded **1998 in Poznań**; the **10,000th store** opened October 2023; a separate **Żabka Nano** self-service format is 24/7. https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Żabka_Polska
- **[UNVERIFIED: standard opening hours and alcohol policy.]** Wikipedia does not state them. **Do not write "Żabka is open 24 hours" as a blanket fact** — only the Nano format is documented as 24/7.

### The night ban (nocna prohibicja) — current and verified
- Since **November 2025**, Warsaw residents cannot buy alcohol in shops or petrol stations after **22:00** in two districts, **Śródmieście** and **Praga Północ**; [VERBATIM] *"Zakaz obowiązuje do godz. 06:00 rano"* — the ban runs until 06:00.
- Legal basis: the **March 2018 amendment** to the 1982 alcohol law, empowering municipalities.
- Night bans have been introduced in **180 gminas**, including **Kraków, Wrocław, Gdańsk, Szczecin and Słupsk**.
- Measured Warsaw effect: police interventions down **37.2%** (137→86); public alcohol-related offences down **42.9%** (891→509).
- Source: https://oko.press/nocna-prohibicja-kiedy-cala-warszawa
> ⚠ **SUPERSEDED — SEE §35.2.** The two-district Warsaw measure was a **pilot**. Warsaw goes **city-wide, 22:00–06:00, from Monday 1 June 2026**, passed 57–2, with the Chopin Airport duty-free zone as the only exemption. §35.1 also carries a verified city-by-city register (Kraków, Wrocław, Gdańsk, Łódź, Poznań, Szczecin, Olsztyn…) with resolution numbers and dates.
- **Note the loop:** a night ban in 2025 recreates the PRL *melina*, now with delivery drivers. Poland has run this experiment twice.

## 15. THE THREE-MEN-SHARE-A-BOTTLE RITUAL — the full documentation

### 15a. The Russian phrase
- Cyrillic: **сообразить на троих** (also *соображать на троих*, *сообразим на троих?*)
- ASCII: **soobrazit na troikh** / soobrazhat na troikh
- **Literal:** *soobrazit* = "to figure out, contrive, arrange"; *na troikh* = "for three". So **"to arrange it for three"**. The verb is deliberately vague — a euphemism. It is **not** "to drink for three".

### 15b. THE CUSTOM WAS MANUFACTURED BY A DECREE. This is the key fact.
Argumenty i Fakty (Belarus edition) records that it arose from a **December 1958** Communist Party anti-alcohol decree banning the sale of vodka **by the glass** in public catering. [VERBATIM Russian]:
> *"вместо скромных ста граммов, которые они могли выпить в относительно культурных условиях, страждущие теперь были вынуждены покупать целую бутылку водки и искать случайных компаньонов"*

Gloss: *instead of the modest hundred grams they could drink in relatively civilised conditions, the thirsty were now forced to buy a whole bottle and look for chance companions.*
Source: https://aif.by/timefree/history/velikiy_alkogolnyy_pochin_otkuda_poyavilsya_obychay_soobrazhat_na_troih

**That is the causal heart of it: the state abolished the single shot, so three strangers had to become a syndicate.** It is not folk tradition; it is a legislative side-effect that hardened into ritual.

### 15c. The economics — every number checked
- **After the 1961 Khrushchev monetary reform, a half-litre of "Московская" cost 2 rubles 87 kopecks.** Life.ru [VERBATIM]: *"После денежной реформы Хрущёва в 1961 году полулитровая бутылка водки «Московская» стоила 2 рубля 87 копеек."* https://life.ru/p/1713294
- **Why 2.87 mattered.** Life.ru [VERBATIM]: *"Это была удобная сумма, идеально делившаяся на троих, — ровно по рублю с человека."* — a convenient sum, dividing perfectly by three: **exactly one ruble a head.**
- **The 13-kopeck remainder.** 3 × 1 rouble = 3.00; 3.00 − 2.87 = **13 kopecks**, which went on the snack. Winestreet [VERBATIM]: *"Остальные копейки, обыкновенно, шли на незамысловатую закуску"* — the remaining kopecks usually went on an unfussy *zakuska*. https://winestreet.ru/article/interesting/1502/
  **[FLAG]** The widely repeated claim that the 13 kopecks bought a **плавленый сырок** (a processed cheese triangle) was **not confirmed verbatim.** Treat the cheese as folklore; the 13 kopecks as arithmetic.
- **Before the reform.** Life.ru [VERBATIM]: *"До реформы цена водки была другой — 21 рубль за бутылку. Тогда компания скидывалась по 7 рублей."* — 21 roubles a bottle, 7 roubles each.
- **Later prices.** AiF: by the early 1970s the cheapest vodka was **3 rubles 62 kopecks**, later **5 rubles 30 kopecks**. **At 3.62 the beautiful one-rouble arithmetic breaks — and the custom outlived its own economics.** That is itself a good line.
- **The share per man.** Life.ru [VERBATIM]: *"На каждого выходило примерно по 166 граммов."* — roughly **166 grams each** (500 ml ÷ 3).

### 15d. The hand signal — two documented versions, do not merge them
1. **Fingers shown from a pocket.** AiF: fingers hidden in the jacket pocket except index and middle (signalling *two* — "I need one more") or index, middle and ring (signalling *three*). https://aif.by/timefree/history/velikiy_alkogolnyy_pochin_otkuda_poyavilsya_obychay_soobrazhat_na_troih
2. **Three fingers at the throat.** Life.ru [VERBATIM]: *"Легендарный жест трёх пальцев у горла или короткая фраза: «По рублю?» — мгновенно объединяли незнакомцев."* — the legendary gesture of three fingers at the throat, or the short phrase ***"По рублю?"*** ("A rouble each?"), instantly united strangers. https://life.ru/p/1713294

**On the throat-flick specifically (щелчок по горлу):** it is a real, separate Russian gesture meaning "let's drink". Its famous origin story — Peter the Great granting a craftsman a branded royal warrant on his neck for free drinks in any tavern — is **explicitly a legend**, and Russian sources also derive it from the 19th-century idiom *заложить за галстук* ("to tuck one behind the necktie"). https://weekend.rambler.ru/read/49718036-otkuda-voznik-schelchok-po-gorlu-prizyvayuschiy-vypit/ ; https://www.shkolazhizni.ru/culture/articles/73509/
> **Do not state the Peter the Great story as history.** Have a character tell it as a story — that is both accurate and characterful.
> **The best-sourced signal for a scene is the finger count, plus the spoken "По рублю?"**

### 15e. How it worked in practice, and the best image in the dossier
AiF describes the sequence: find a discreet spot; divide the vodka fast into equal shares; drink within seconds; get rid of the bottle and hide the glass to avoid the police. (Same AiF URL.)

And when there were no matching glasses, the division was done **by ear**. Life.ru [VERBATIM]:
> *"Когда под рукой не было одинаковых стаканов, приходилось наливать «по булькам» — считали звуки, которые водка издавала при розливе."*

Gloss: *When there were no identical glasses to hand, they had to pour **«по булькам»** — "by the glugs" — counting the sounds the vodka made as it was poured.*
Source: https://life.ru/p/1713294
> ***«По булькам»*** (po bulkam) — **three men, one bottle, no measure, counting glugs.** This is the single best-verified image in the whole file.

### 15g. The Russian farewell drink — the parallel to *strzemienny*
***на посошок*** (*na pososhok*, "for the little staff") — the last drink before departure. ### THE VESSEL — the best physical object in the dossier
The glass used was a ***kusztyk*** (also *kuśtyk*, or *kulawka*). obcyjezykpolski.pl describes it [VERBATIM] as being *"płaskiej podstawy lub z nóżką, ale bez podstawy"* — **without a flat base, or with a stem but no base** — so it **could not be set down**, and the drinker had to empty it at once. Same URL. sjp.pl's entry likewise describes a final toast drunk from horseback from a special cup held in the palm. https://sjp.pl/strzemienny
> **A glass engineered so that you cannot put it down until it is empty.** Note also the name ***kulawka*** — from *kulawy*, "lame, limping". **A lame glass.**

**Literary attestation [VERIFIED]:** **Juliusz Słowacki** wrote of *"wielkie strzemienne podano kielichy"* — great stirrup cups were served — and mentions smaller *kusztyczki*. https://obcyjezykpolski.pl/strzemiennego/

### The order of toasts — what IS documented
**[VERIFIED]** from *Encyklopedia staropolska* (Wikiźródła), the order at a noble feast: [VERBATIM] *"Najprzód więc wznoszono zdrowie Najjaśniejszej Rzeczypospolitej, potem Króla Jego-Mości"* — first the health of the Most Serene Commonwealth, then His Majesty the King — then the Queen, then the Primate or bishop, then the most distinguished guest, then the clergy, then the hosts. At name-days one began with the ***solenizant***; at weddings with the ***państwo młodzi*** (the newlyweds).
**The feast closed with the toast ***"Kochajmy się!"*** ("Let us love one another!"), sometimes called ***"sandomierskie"***.** Only after that came ***strzemienne***, drunk on the porch or at the gate, foot in the stirrup.
https://pl.wikisource.org/wiki/Encyklopedia_staropolska/Toasty
> ***"Kochajmy się!"* is also the closing line of Mickiewicz's *Pan Tadeusz*.** That resonance is available and it is real.

**The modern equivalent [VERIFIED as far as two links]:** *strzemienny* ↔ ***rozchodniak*** / ***rozchodniaczek*** (from *rozchodzić się*, to disperse) — given as direct synonyms by Wikisłownik and named as the contemporary counterpart by obcyjezykpolski.pl. https://pl.wiktionary.org/wiki/rozchodniak
- **[UNVERIFIED: any longer named ladder of successive "one more" drinks.]** What is supportable is: **… → "Kochajmy się!" (a.k.a. "sandomierskie") → strzemienne**, with *rozchodniak / rozchodniaczek* as the modern synonym of the last one. **Do not invent extra rungs.**
- **REGISTER — do not get this backwards.** *"Strzemiennego!"* is **archaic-jocular**: a modern Pole says it knowingly, with a smile, as a period flourish. ***Rozchodniaczek*** is the ordinary living word. **A 1975 bench drinker says *rozchodniaczek*. A self-consciously literary host says *strzemiennego*.**

## 20. TOAST RULES AND ETIQUETTE — several common beliefs REJECTED

### ✅ VERIFIED
**1. You stand for a toast.** *Encyklopedia staropolska* [VERBATIM]: *"Pijąc zdrowie, wstawano"* — drinking a health, one stood; the proposer spoke before drinking. *(Historical/formal register — not the rule at a modern kitchen table.)*

**2. Refusing a toast is an offence — and there was machinery to prevent it.** Muzeum Polskiej Wódki records that refusing to join a toast was considered offensive to the person honoured, giving rise to ***przynuka*** — mutual urging and pressure to drink. https://muzeumpolskiejwodki.pl/aktualnosci/na-pierwsza-nozke-podstawy-polskich-toastow/

**3. "Do dna" is a real expectation.** The phrase ***do dna*** ("to the bottom") reflects the expectation of finishing the drink. The same source gives the brutal old benchmark that a successful banquet was one where [VERBATIM] *"większość męskich gości nie mogła wyjść z niego o własnych siłach"* — **most of the male guests could not leave under their own power.** https://miedzymostowe.blogspot.com/2018/02/a-kto-nie-wypije-tego-we-dwa-kije-czyli.html
*(Note: *do dna* is not a dictionary headword — sjp.pl/do+dna returns 404 — but the practice and phrase are attested in cultural literature. Use the phrase; don't cite a dictionary.)*
**The blog's own title is a verified Polish rhyme worth having: *"A kto nie wypije, tego we dwa kije"*** — *and whoever doesn't drink up, him with two sticks*. Same URL.

**4. Coercion was structural, not merely social.** The same source documents ***przymus propinacyjny*** — the feudal obligation on peasants to buy alcohol produced on the lord's estate — and ***przymus konsumpcyjny***, requiring households to buy set quantities annually. **Polish drinking culture has a coercive economic skeleton, not just a convivial one. That is a serious point and it is documented.**

**5. Women's expected behaviour (HISTORICAL).** Same source: women practised restraint at feasts, **merely touching their lips to the glass**, their sex being expected to show *"wstrzemięźliwość"*. **This is a historical norm, verified as such. Do not project it onto a modern scene without intending to.**

**6. The tavern as a rival institution.** Same source: the ***karczma*** functioned as a social institution rivalling the Church in village life, hosting weddings, christenings and funerals.

**7. Modern glass sizes — and a counter-intuitive modern rule.** A Polish drinks guide states pure vodka is drunk *"z kieliszków o pojemności 35-40 ml"* (35–40 ml glasses); flavoured vodka from glasses *"nie przekracza 25 ml"*; and — notably — that at formal occasions *"wódką nie powinniśmy wznosić toastu"*, **vodka should not be used to raise the toast** (sparkling or red wine instead). https://winnicalidla.pl/blog/jak-pic-wodke-poradnik.html
> ⚠️ **This 35–40 ml figure contradicts the widely repeated "Polish shot = 50 ml", which could NOT be verified from any source fetched.** If a number must appear in the text, use 35–40 ml with this citation, or avoid a figure.

## 21. THE BAR MLECZNY — AND AN IMPORTANT CORRECTION

**The brief grouped the *bar mleczny* with the *monopolowy* and provincial nightlife. That grouping is wrong and it matters.**

**A *bar mleczny* is by legal definition ALCOHOL-FREE.** Polish Wikipedia's opening sentence, [VERBATIM], citing the Social Assistance Act of 12 March 2004:
> *"Bar mleczny – działalność gospodarcza w postaci samoobsługowych, **bezalkoholowych**, ogólnodostępnych zakładów masowego żywienia, sprzedających całodziennie posiłki mleczno-nabiałowo-jarskie"*

Gloss: *Milk bar — a business in the form of self-service, **alcohol-free**, generally accessible mass-catering establishments selling milk-, dairy- and vegetable-based meals all day.*
Source: https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_mleczny (raw wikitext fetched 17.08.2026)

**So the milk bar is the sober institution. It is where you eat, cheaply, when you have no money — not where you drink.** If the book wants the drinking counterpart, that is the *monopolowy*, the *melina*/*meta*, the shop wall, or the *knajpa* / *pub* / *piwiarnia*.
**One real exception, and it is a good detail [VERBATIM]:** *"W latach pięćdziesiątych XX w. w niektórych barach mlecznych oferowano także piwo."* — **in the 1950s some milk bars also served beer.** That stopped.

### Verified history
- **The first milk bar opened in Warsaw in 1896**, by **Stanisław Dłużewski**, a dairy-cattle breeder, farmer and landowner. It was the *"mleczarnia »Nadświdrzańska«"* with a garden, on **ul. Nowy Świat**, on the site later occupied by the Bank Gospodarstwa Krajowego building. It was profitable and imitators followed.
- **By 1918 the idea had spread across the country.** In the Depression years popularity rose further, and **ministerial regulations set the size, composition and prices of dishes** so they would remain affordable for the poor. **The price control is older than communism.**
- **Under the PRL the form expanded intensively.** Most milk bars belonged to the consumer co-operative ***"Społem"***.
- **Menu:** predominantly milk dishes, plus egg dishes (omelettes), groats (*kasza*), flour-based dishes (*pierogi*); meat much more rarely.

### The physical texture, verified — use this, it is exact
[VERBATIM]:
> *"Dla barów mlecznych okresu PRL charakterystyczne były talerze i kubki z grubej ceramiki z niebieskimi obwódkami, często wyszczerbione, sztućce z odlewu aluminiowego, stoliki z blatami z laminatu bez obrusów i krzesła o konstrukcji spawanej z prętów stalowych. Wskutek słabej wentylacji w sali konsumpcyjnej unosiły się zapachy kuchenne."*

Gloss: *Characteristic of PRL-era milk bars were thick ceramic plates and mugs with blue rims, often chipped; cast-aluminium cutlery; laminate-topped tables with no tablecloths; and chairs welded from steel rod. Because of poor ventilation, cooking smells hung in the dining room.*

And [VERBATIM]: *"W barach mlecznych ceny są często podawane z dokładnością do jednego grosza – bez zaokrągleń."* — **prices are often given to the nearest single grosz, unrounded.** (A *grosz* is 1/100 of a złoty. A price board reading 4,37 zł.)

### The Bareja joke — verified
[VERBATIM]: *"Ironiczny, negatywnie przejaskrawiony wizerunek baru mlecznego z przełomu lat 70. i 80. XX wieku, z **aluminiowymi talerzami przytwierdzonymi do blatów stołów i łyżkami na łańcuchach**, przedstawiony został w komedii Stanisława Barei »Miś«."*

Gloss: *An ironic, negatively exaggerated image of the turn-of-the-1970s/80s milk bar, **with aluminium plates bolted to the tabletops and spoons on chains**, was presented in Stanisław Bareja's comedy "Miś".*

**Plates bolted down and spoons on chains, because otherwise they get stolen.** This is the single most famous PRL milk-bar image in Polish culture, it comes from *Miś* (1981), and Poles quote it constantly. **Note the Wikipedia framing calls it "negatively exaggerated" — i.e. satire, not documentary. Do not present it as literal fact; present it as the joke Poles tell.**
Wikipedia also compares the milk bar's place in Polish consciousness to the American **Automat**.

### They still exist, and the state still pays — verified with figures
- [VERBATIM] *"Bary mleczne są często również wspierane przez miasta, które zapewniają np. preferencyjne stawki czynszu"* — supported by cities with preferential rents.
- **Central subsidy is a live legal instrument.** Governed by the **Regulation of the Minister of Finance of 30 March 2015** on subject-matter subsidies for meals sold in milk bars. To qualify, a business had to apply a **mark-up of no more than 56%** of the value of raw materials, and to display the sign ***"bar mleczny"*** on the premises; **from 01.06.2022 the mark-up cap was reduced to 45%.**
- **An earlier regulation, amended in December 2014, allowed only subsidised raw materials in subsidised dishes — and the finance minister did not put SPICES on the list of subsidised raw materials.** [VERBATIM] *"na liście takich surowców minister finansów nie umieścił przypraw"*.
  **A state-subsidised restaurant legally discouraged from seasoning the food. That is a whole essay in one clause, and it is verified.**
- **Subsidy paid:** **2018 — 15.46 m zł; 2019 — 17.62 m zł; 2020 — 12.28 m zł; 2021 — 11.23 m zł.**
- Prices in milk bars are noted as often **three times lower** than the same dish in a restaurant.

All the above from https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_mleczny

## 22. RATIONING AND ALCOHOL UNDER THE PRL — verified dates

Source for this section: https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reglamentacja_towarów_w_Polsce_Ludowej (raw wikitext fetched 17.08.2026)

- **Post-war rationing was fully abolished on 1 January 1949** — Poland was, per the article, only the third country to do so after Switzerland and the USSR, and the decision was political (1948, the founding of the PZPR) rather than economic. Fat coupons (*bony tłuszczowe*) were reintroduced immediately afterwards and ran to mid-1950.
- **Rationing returned in 1976** (after the June 1976 events) under Piotr Jaroszewicz's government, initially printed with banknote-grade security features, later increasingly simplified. Sugar rationing ran **until 1 November 1985 — almost ten years.**
- **Meat rationing** was one of the 21 Gdańsk demands of August 1980 and was actually introduced on **28 February 1981**.
- **30 April 1981** — extended to all meat products, butter, flour, rice and groats.
- **1 September 1981** — extended to soap and washing powder.
- **After martial law was declared on 13 December 1981**, the card system was progressively extended to further groups of goods — [VERBATIM] *"m.in. czekoladę, **alkohol**, benzynę i wiele innych"* — **including chocolate, ALCOHOL and petrol** — reaching, in the article's words, a scope *"znacznie szerszy niż w czasie okupacji podczas II wojny światowej"* — **considerably wider than under the German occupation during the Second World War.**

**So: alcohol ration cards in Poland date from the martial-law period, 1981 onwards.**
> ⚠ **SOURCES CONFLICT — SEE §34.1.** IPN historian **Dr Andrzej Zawistowski** puts alcohol in the **April 1981** expansion, i.e. *before* martial law; PAP and IPN Rzeszów agree with the Wikipedia dating used above. Write "1981" and note the dispute.
> **★ AND THE RATION SIZE IS NOW VERIFIED: half a litre of vodka OR one bottle of imported wine, per person per month — with supplementary coupons issued for weddings and christenings.** See §34.1. Set it against the proverb at §25: *"Chrzest polewaj wodą, wesele – wódką…"*

**Two crucial mechanics, both verified:**
- [VERBATIM] *"W PRL kartki jedynie upoważniały do zakupu towarów, tzn. za towary płaciło się normalnymi pieniędzmi; kartki nie zastępowały środków płatniczych."* — **the cards only ENTITLED you to buy; you still paid with ordinary money. They were not currency.** (This is the detail foreigners always get wrong.)
- Separate from the cards were ***bony dolarowe*** — dollar vouchers, a substitute means of payment equivalent to the US dollar, and the ***bon towarowy PeKaO*** used in the hard-currency **Pewex** shops.

**Two more PRL words the article gives, both real:**
- ***stacz kolejkowy*** [stacz kolejkowy] — **a professional queue-stander**, a man paid to hold your place in a line.
- ***sprzedaż wiązana*** [sprzedaz wiazana] — **tied selling**: to get the thing you want you must also buy the thing nobody wants.

**And the surreal apex, [VERBATIM]:** at the peak of rationing, purchase entitlements were recorded in a **child's health booklet** (*książeczka zdrowia dziecka*), in which shops were obliged to stamp every purchase of cotton wool, nappies, powdered milk, butter etc.; and receipts from **waste-paper collection points** entitled the bearer to buy toilet paper at **one roll per kilogram of waste paper**.

## 23. BEER VS VODKA — THE LONG ARC, FROM PRIMARY SOURCES

Source for this section unless stated: **Polish Wikipedia, "Piwo w Polsce"** — https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piwo_w_Polsce (raw wikitext fetched 17.08.2026).

### 23a. The important thing almost nobody outside Poland knows: POLAND WAS A BEER COUNTRY FIRST

[VERBATIM]:
> *"Piwo, które wówczas nie miało wysokiej zawartości alkoholu, pili magnaci i zamożna szlachta – nawet kilkaset litrów na rok, jak również pospólstwo. W Rzeczypospolitej podczas uroczystości szlacheckich jeszcze w XVI–XVII wieku poczęstunek popijano głównie piwem. Wino podawano po pierwszej części uczty."*

Gloss: *Beer, which then had a low alcohol content, was drunk by magnates and the wealthy szlachta — even several hundred litres a year — as well as by the common people. In the Commonwealth, at noble ceremonies as late as the 16th–17th centuries, the food was washed down mainly with beer. Wine was served after the first part of the feast.*

### 23b. HOW VODKA WON — and the mechanism is agricultural, not cultural

[VERBATIM]:
> *"Ze względu na liczne wojny w XVII w., **nadmiar zboża, z którego masowo wyrabiano tanią wódkę**, wzrost cen dobrego piwa i pogorszenie się jakości piw tanich, piwo w Polsce zaczęło powoli tracić swoją pozycję. Do upadku staropolskiej tradycji piwnej przyczyniła się ostatecznie fatalna polityczno-ekonomiczna sytuacja Polski oraz **rozbiory**, które z jednej strony doprowadziły do upadku jakości polskiego piwa, a z drugiej spowodowały zalew Polski piwami importowanymi."*

Gloss: *Because of the numerous 17th-century wars, **a surplus of grain from which cheap vodka was mass-produced**, rising prices for good beer and falling quality of cheap beers, beer in Poland slowly began to lose its position. The collapse of the Old Polish beer tradition was finally brought about by Poland's disastrous political-economic situation and **the partitions**, which on one hand destroyed the quality of Polish beer and on the other flooded Poland with imported beers.*

**So the story is: a grain glut on the noble estates made distilling profitable, and the estate then had a legal instrument to force the peasants to buy the product (*przymus propinacyjny*, §20). Polish vodka culture has an agricultural-economic origin, not an innate one.** This is the single most useful corrective in the whole file against the "Poles just drink" cliché — and it dovetails exactly with the *przymus propinacyjny* finding at §20 and with the Soviet-decree origin of the three-man share at §15b. **Three times in this dossier, a "national drinking custom" turns out to have been engineered by somebody's balance sheet.**

Note also the World-War-I collapse [VERBATIM]: *"wraz z I wojną światową nastąpiło załamanie przemysłu piwowarskiego, spadek jakości spowodowany głównie niedostatkiem surowców oraz zmniejszenie spożycia piwa."*

### 23c. THE POST-1989 BEER BOOM — verified

[VERBATIM]:
> *"Ponowny rozwój piwowarstwa nastąpił dopiero **w latach 90. XX wieku, kiedy znacjonalizowane w czasach PRL-u browary zaczęto prywatyzować**. Rozwój gospodarki wolnorynkowej doprowadził do zainteresowania polskim rynkiem browarniczym największych światowych grup piwowarskich. Doprowadziło to z jednej strony do **upadku wielu małych i średnich browarów**, ale również do konsolidacji i rozwoju największych browarów oraz **radykalnego wzrostu spożycia piwa przez Polaków**."*

Gloss: *A renewed development of brewing came only **in the 1990s, when breweries nationalised under the PRL began to be privatised**. The free-market economy drew the world's largest brewing groups to the Polish market. This led on the one hand to **the collapse of many small and medium breweries**, but also to the consolidation and growth of the largest breweries and **a radical rise in beer consumption by Poles**.*

**That is the verified core of the generational shift: privatisation of the state breweries in the 1990s → foreign capital → consolidation → a radical rise in beer drinking.** The mechanism named by the source is ownership and capital, not advertising — **[UNVERIFIED: the specific claim that the 1990s liberalisation of beer ADVERTISING drove the shift. It is plausible and widely repeated. It is not in this source. Check it separately.]**

### 23e. WHAT POLES DRINK NOW — and the sober turn is in the data

**What the series says, and it is not what most people expect:**
- **Vodka's share as the preferred drink fell by a third between 2007 and 2019** (24% → 16%).

- **The growth is in wine and in premium spirits** — whisky and cognac more than doubled (5% → 11%). **The story since 2010 is not "beer replaced vodka"; it is "the whole thing bourgeoisified".**
- [VERBATIM] the report speculates that the growing "something else" category *"Być może kryją się za nią cydry albo radlery czy shandy, czyli miksy piwa i lemoniady"* — cider, radlers, shandies.

- **By occupation:** *"Spośród grup zawodowych po wódkę najczęściej sięgają robotnicy wykwalifikowani (24%) i niewykwalifikowani (22%), emeryci (25%) oraz renciści (26%). It's also a susceptibility to reaching for the bottle. On the other hand — the market doesn't care for alcohol. It abolished the principle that you could drink for a long time and bear no consequences. **In the old days, on a work brigade, if one man was drunk on Tuesday the brigade covered for him, and on Wednesday he made it up.** And so on. Today people don't want to work for someone else, and employers sack you for drunkenness because productivity falls. You can't be effective turning up to work with a permanent hangover. **The market economy destroyed the status of the drunken sacred cow, which was well established under the PRL.***

> **"Pijana święta krowa" — the drunken sacred cow — is the single most useful phrase in this section, and the brigade covering for its Tuesday drunk is the single most useful image.** This is the real mechanism of the post-1989 change: not advertising, not taste, but the end of a labour regime that could absorb drunkenness. Osiatyński is a serious source (constitutional lawyer, and himself a recovering alcoholic and author on addiction — **[BG, verify]**), and his headline is explicitly an attack on PRL nostalgia about drinking.

## 28. THE "POLAK PIJAK" STEREOTYPE — what this research actually supports

**[HONEST STATUS: I did not find a dedicated Polish-language source analysing the "Polak pijak" stereotype and Polish resentment of it. That specific brief item is NOT delivered.]** What the verified material above does let the book say, with citations:

1. **Poles were beer drinkers first.** Vodka displaced beer in the 17th century because of a grain surplus on noble estates and the legal power to force peasants to buy the product. §23a–23b, §20.
2. **The coercion was economic and legal, not temperamental** — *przymus propinacyjny*, *przymus konsumpcyjny*, the state spirits monopoly of 1924, the excise regime. §20, §14, §16.
3. **The most famous "Slavic" drinking ritual of all — three men sharing a bottle — was manufactured by a 1958 Soviet decree.** §15b.
4. **The consumption data do not support a picture of ever-increasing drinking.** Between 2007 and 2019 vodka's share as the preferred drink fell by a third; beer's share fell sharply after 2010; heavy drinkers and total abstainers BOTH declined as the moderate middle grew; and among 18–24s, men's drinking fell while women's rose. §24.
5. **But the same official document records three million *małpki* sold daily, a third of them before noon** — so the sober trend and the furtive 200 ml bottle are simultaneous, not sequential. §24h.
6. **And the state's own pollster says in print that it failed to capture the people with a serious problem.** §24d.
7. **The most incisive Polish account of what changed after 1989 is Osiatyński's**, and it is about labour discipline, not morals: the market killed the "drunken sacred cow" that the work brigade used to cover for. §26.
8. **Poland's own self-reproach has a proverb and a date**: *"Jak za króla Sasa. Jedz, pij i popuszczaj pasa."* §25.

**Every one of those eight is sourced in this file. That is a stronger and less clichéd account of Polish drinking than "Poles drink a lot", and it is the account the book should run on.**

## 31. THE SIX THINGS TO ACTUALLY WRITE WITH

If the book takes only six things out of this file, take these. All are verified and all are load-bearing.

1. **«По булькам».** Three men, one bottle, no glasses, dividing the vodka by counting the glugs. Manufactured by a 1958 decree that abolished the single shot. One rouble each out of 2.87, thirteen kopecks left for the snack. §15.
2. **The *kusztyk* / *kulawka*** — the stirrup cup with no base, which cannot be set down until it is empty. A *lame* glass. And *strzemienny*, the drink taken with your foot already in the stirrup. §18.
3. **The monopolowy that opened at 10:00 and could sell nothing until 13:00.** A shop that sold only alcohol, forbidden to sell for the first three hours of its day, in the 1980s. §14.
4. **The Vigor reveal.** Two Poles swigging from the bottle in Lviv market square, thinking they look magnificent, photographed by everyone — because it is erectile-dysfunction medicine. Then the girl's speech: *"Here you make up for the fact that everywhere else they wipe their asses with you."* Then: *"Here," I said, handing them the Vigor. "Drink up."* §5c.
5. **"W każdym normalnym kraju… nie używa się zwrotu »w każdym normalnym kraju«."** And its companion, from Szczerek's own childhood: *"if Copernicus was a Pole, why not Columbus?"* §4e, §8.
6. **"Elaborate chaos."** Szczerek's actual position, not the sneer he is accused of: *"If Poland's trademark has to be the ubiquitous roadside signboards and advertising chaos, why not make them nice signboards and elaborate chaos?"* Plus the *Siódemka* epitaph: *"It may be ugly but it's ours and it feels like home, no matter how annoying."* §9d.

**And three more that arrived late but earn their place:**

7. **Three million *małpki* a day, a third of them before noon** — in the same paragraph of the same official CBOS document that reports the rise of alcohol-free beer. Poland got more sober and more furtive at once. §24h.
8. **The drunken sacred cow.** Osiatyński: on a PRL work brigade, if one man was drunk on Tuesday the brigade covered for him and he made it up on Wednesday; *"gospodarka rynkowa zniszczyła status pijanej świętej krowy"* — the market economy destroyed that. §26.
9. ***"A kto nie wypije, tego we dwa kije."*** *And whoever doesn't drink up — him with two sticks.* A proverb, and a line from a drinking song, so it is sung by the very people it threatens. §25.

## 32. THE ARGUMENT ABOUT SZCZEREK, IN THREE SENTENCES

**The charge:** he is a *turysta z tezą* — a tourist with a thesis — who carts his clichés from town to town, renders real places invisible, and lets metropolitan readers enjoy the province as spectacle while believing they are receiving truth (Michał R. Wiśniewski, 2014); and although he performs the orientalising gaze in order to satirise it, the prose orientalises anyway, and readers absorb the images, not the irony (Adam Balcer, 2025). §7a, §7b.

**The defence:** he is from Radom, not from Warsaw; *Mordor* declares its own unreliability and is structurally a book about the dishonesty of the genre it is written in; the Jędrzejów paragraph calls the town a rubbish tip AND says its fury is earned in the same breath; his first response to Ukraine on record is recognition of his own childhood, not contempt; and the argument he actually makes is that Poles should stop being ashamed of their landscape and start building on it. §7d, §9b, §9d.

**The honest verdict for a book to take:** both are true at once, and that is precisely why he is worth writing about — the gonzo method buys the access and the drinking that make the reportage possible, and charges for it in exactly the coin his critics name. He arranges his own humiliation (the Vigor label) more reliably than he arranges anyone else's, which is the strongest thing that can be said in his favour, and it is not a complete answer to Wiśniewski.

### ★ THE HEADLINE FINDING — and it corrects the popular story AND my §23
- **Beer overtook spirits in 1998** (42.5% vs 34.2%). In 1997 spirits were still marginally ahead. **Not 1989, not "the early 90s" — 1998.**

### 37.2 ★ THE FINDING THAT REFRAMES THE WHOLE BEER-VS-VODKA STORY
Kosiński's structural series, VERBATIM: *"w 1929 r. wśród alkoholi wypijanych przez mieszkańców Drugiej Rzeczypospolitej wódka stanowiła 88%, piwo – 11%, a wino zaledwie 1%. …Na przykład w 1933 r. wódka stanowiła 92% wypitego alkoholu, piwo – 8%."* Still 92% vodka in 1948. Then:
1951 — 81/13/6 · 1956 — 74/13/13 · **1965 — 63.1% vodka / 14.6% wine / 22.3% beer** · 1970 — 63.1/13.7/23.2 · 1975 — 66.7/13/20.3 · **1980 — 70.6% vodka / 15% wine / 14.4% beer** · 1980s stable at *"wódki wynosił średnio około 67–68%, wina – około 13–16%, piwa – 17–18%."*

> **Beer's share of Polish drinking FELL through the Gierek decade — from 23.2% in 1970 to 14.4% in 1980. The 1990s beer boom was not the continuation of a trend. It was a reversal of one.** Set this beside §23a (Poland as a beer country until the 17th century) and the arc is: beer → vodka (17th c., grain surplus + propinacja) → beer creeping back (1950s–60s) → vodka reasserting (1970s) → beer (1998–2012) → spirits again (2002–present). **Nothing about Polish drinking is stable, and every turn has an economic cause.**

**The "polski wzór picia"** — Kosiński credits the term to **Jan K. Falewicz**, *Jak pijemy?*, from 1960s OBOP/SKP research. Six features: dominance of vodka; early initiation (18–24); very large single sittings; high frequency; group drinking among trusted intimates; and drinking ***na czczo*** — on an empty stomach.

**★ On the *zakąska* (corrects the romantic picture at §17), VERBATIM:** *"Wśród mężczyzn i w mieście, i na wsi zakąska do mocniejszych trunków (wódki) okazywała się nierzadko… **piwo**."* — **15.3% of urban and 17.5% of rural men used beer as the chaser; only 2.4–2.7% of women.**

**Social pressure, VERBATIM:** *"»Jest taka atmosfera, że trzeba pić« – komentowała to zjawisko posłanka Kluszyńska w lipcu 1952 r."* And in 1968, **40.2% of urban men, 38.8% of rural men, 42.3% of urban women and 39.9% of rural women admitted drinking *"z obowiązku towarzyskiego"*** — out of social obligation.

**The stock phrase, VERBATIM:** *"»No to jak, oblewamy?« – brzmiało jedno z charakterystycznych wyrażeń języka potocznego czasów PRL."*
**Workplace ritual vocabulary, VERBATIM:** *"obyczaj »stawiania« majstrowi, »opijania« pierwszej wypłaty, czy »wkupywania się« za pomocą fundowanego alkoholu do robotniczej społeczności."*
> **Four verbs to steal: *oblewać* (to "wet" an occasion), *stawiać* (to stand a round, esp. to the foreman), *opijać* (to drink to something — the first pay packet), *wkupywać się* (to buy your way into a group with drink).** All four are current Polish.

### 38.4 ★★ POBŁOCKI, *CHAMSTWO* — the deep historical frame my §23b was reaching for
**Kacper Pobłocki, *Chamstwo*, Wydawnictwo Czarne, Wołowiec 2021.** Quoted on pl.wikiquote (*Karczma*, *Pijaństwo*), VERBATIM:

> *"Wódka świetnie uzupełniała praktykę zapominania o frasunku poprzez taniec. Państwo o tym wiedzieli, stopniowo zamieniając karczmy głównie w miejsca spożycia alkoholu. **Trzysta lat pańszczyzny to również trzysta lat konsekwentnego rozpijania polskiego ludu.** Wódka stała się wręcz najbardziej dochodową gałęzią pańskiej gospodarki – »prawdziwą mennicą«, jak to ujął książę Czartoryski."* (pp. ~263–264)

Gloss: *Vodka perfectly complemented the practice of forgetting one's sorrows through dance. The lords knew this, gradually turning taverns mainly into places of alcohol consumption. **Three hundred years of serfdom were also three hundred years of the consistent getting-drunk of the Polish people.** Vodka became outright the most profitable branch of the manorial economy — "a real mint", as Prince Czartoryski put it.*

**And on the temperance oaths, VERBATIM:** *"W latach 1844–1855 setki tysięcy galicyjskich poddanych złożyło publiczną, uroczystą przysięgę, że przestaną pić. W niektórych obwodach zdecydowało się na to nawet trzy czwarte dorosłych. …Głównym agitatorem był lokalny kler. …Panowie bronili propinacji jako swojej »stałej, integralnej, nieodzownej części dochodów«… Gdy dwa lata później powstała wielogłowa galicyjska hurma, **panów rżnięto na trzeźwo**; dyscypliny pilnował między innymi Szela."*

Gloss: *In 1844–1855 hundreds of thousands of Galician subjects swore a public, solemn oath to stop drinking. In some districts as many as three-quarters of adults did so. …The chief agitator was the local clergy. …The lords defended propinacja as their "permanent, integral, indispensable share of income"… When two years later the many-headed Galician mob rose, **the lords were slaughtered sober**; discipline was kept by, among others, Szela.*

> **"Panów rżnięto na trzeźwo" — the lords were slaughtered sober. That is the single most extraordinary sentence in this entire dossier.** The 1846 Galician slaughter (*rabacja galicyjska*, Jakub Szela) followed a mass temperance movement, and Pobłocki's point is that sobriety was the precondition of the revolt. **[BG: the rabacja is 1846, Galicia, and Szela its leader — standard Polish history, not verified this session.]**

And VERBATIM, Joseph August Schultes via Pobłocki p. 263: *"W Galicji słońce tylko po to zdaje się świecić, by wskazywać chłopu drogę do karczmy."* — *In Galicia the sun seems to shine only to show the peasant the way to the tavern.*

> **★ THIS IS THE FOUNDATION THE WHOLE DRINKING BOOK SHOULD SIT ON.** ***Propinacja*** — the nobility's legal monopoly on distilling and selling drink in its own villages, with the peasants legally obliged to buy — makes Polish "national" drunkenness a documented product of a rent-extraction system. It is the same mechanism as *przymus propinacyjny* at §20, now with a book, a duration (three centuries), a quotation from a Czartoryski, and a mass sobriety movement that ended in a massacre.
> **And note: *Chamstwo* is published by Wydawnictwo Czarne — Szczerek's own publisher.**

## 42. THE REVISED "BEST OF" — supersedes §31

Ten items, all verified, ranked for a book about drinking:

1. **"Panów rżnięto na trzeźwo."** The lords were slaughtered sober. Pobłocki, *Chamstwo* — three hundred years of *propinacja*, a mass temperance movement in Galicia 1844–55, and then 1846. §38.4.
2. **"Brak potrzeby" — 79%.** The top reason Polish 18–24-year-olds give for not drinking is *no need*. CBOS 81/2026. §40.1.
3. **«По булькам».** Three men, one bottle, no glasses, dividing the vodka by counting the glugs — a custom manufactured by a 1958 decree. §15.
4. **The ration and the proverb.** Martial-law Poland issued half a litre of vodka per person per month, *with supplementary coupons for weddings and christenings* — and the proverb runs *"Chrzest polewaj wodą, wesele – wódką, a pogrzeb – łzami."* §34.1, §25.
5. **"Na szklanki nie podajemy. Może być pięćdziesiątka albo pół litra."** *Alternatywy 4*, 1983 — the state abolishing the glass as a unit, played as comedy. §38.2.
6. **The 1999–2002 surrogate.** Polish brewers legally advertised beer by pretending it was alcohol-free; in 2026 the Sejm proposes banning alcohol-free beer advertising for real. §34.3, §40.3.
7. **The Vigor reveal.** Szczerek's narrator discovers mid-swagger that his drink is erectile-dysfunction medicine, then hands the bottle on: *"Here," I said, handing them the Vigor. "Drink up."* §5c.
8. **90 bottles then, 238 now.** What a Polish monthly wage buys in vodka, 2002 vs 2024. §36.2.
9. **The drunken sacred cow.** On a PRL work brigade one man could be drunk on Tuesday and make it up on Wednesday; *"gospodarka rynkowa zniszczyła status pijanej świętej krowy"* — Osiatyński. §26.
10. **The Croatian speech from *Wesele*.** A drunk Pole at a wedding telling a camera that foreigners drink better: *"A jak się napiją, to się nie napierdalają, tylko się bawią i śpiewają."* §38.2.

**And one structural argument the book should run on, now supported at every point:**
> **Every "national" Polish drinking custom in this dossier turns out to have been engineered by somebody's balance sheet.** *Propinacja* and the grain surplus made vodka displace beer in the 17th century (§23b, §38.4). The Soviet decree of 1958 created the three-man share (§15b). The PZPR's 13:00 rule created the queue (§34.2). Martial law created the vodka coupon (§34.1). Heineken, SABMiller and Carlsberg created the beer boom (§36.1), and the 2001 advertising law let them sell it (§34.3). The 2021 fee and the 2018 night bans are the state trying to un-engineer it (§40.4, §35).
> **Nobody in this file simply drinks because they are Polish.**
